## U.S. National News

**Nation commemorates the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks.** Memorial observances in New York, at the Pentagon and near Shanksville, Pennsylvania, include moments of silence and readings of the names of nearly 3,000 people killed in 2001. President Donald Trump attended the Pentagon ceremony, where the names of the 184 people killed there were read aloud.

**August inflation holds at 3.4% as fuel costs climb.** The consumer price index rose 0.4% from July and 3.4% from a year earlier, while core inflation eased to 2.4% year over year. Gasoline rose 3.9% during the month, and the report increased expectations that the Federal Reserve may raise interest rates next week.

**Appeals court keeps block on mail-voting restrictions in place.** A three-judge federal appeals panel declined to lift an injunction against an executive order that would impose new federal requirements on mailed ballots. The ruling comes as several states begin sending ballots for the November midterm elections and the Supreme Court considers the dispute.

**Federal appeals courts remain divided over mandatory immigrant detention.** The 4th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled that holding immigrants arrested inside the country without access to bond hearings is illegal. Nine federal appeals courts have now rejected the policy and two have upheld it, increasing the likelihood of Supreme Court review.

**Kauai asks tourists to postpone visits during hurricane recovery.** Hawaii tourism officials urged visitors to avoid Kauai while crews repair roads, restore power and clear damage caused by Hurricane Lowell's winds and storm surge. Thousands of customers were still without electricity late Thursday.

## International News

**Houthi forces seize strategic Red Sea island.** Yemen's Iranian-backed Houthis captured Mayun Island at the southern entrance to the Red Sea, according to officials from both sides of the conflict. The move places new pressure on the Bab el-Mandeb shipping route and could threaten Saudi oil exports.

**Poland warns of possible Russian provocations at Ukraine's European borders.** Prime Minister Donald Tusk said incidents near crossings with Poland and Moldova may foreshadow more attacks, although he presented no public evidence for all aspects of the allegation. Two people were reported killed when Russian drones struck the Starokozache crossing between Ukraine and Moldova on Tuesday.

**BRICS leaders gather in New Delhi amid deep geopolitical divisions.** India is hosting leaders from the expanded 11-country group this weekend, including the presidents of China, Russia and Iran. Wars in Ukraine and the Middle East, along with U.S.-China tensions, will test the bloc's ability to agree on common positions.

**Nigeria suspends parliamentary exchanges with South Africa.** Nigeria's National Assembly halted official visits in response to anti-immigrant violence in South Africa that has targeted migrants from other African countries. South African government figures say nearly 90,000 people have been repatriated or deported since the tensions began.

**Brazil's Supreme Court crisis disrupts its work before elections.** The court canceled a second consecutive session as justices face allegations involving graft, political bias and police interference. The full court is scheduled to meet Tuesday to consider whether Justice Alexandre de Moraes should be investigated over alleged links to the owner of failed Banco Master.

## Technology Industry News

**IDScan confirms theft of more than 150 million driver's-license images.** The identity-verification provider acknowledged that attackers stole a large collection of license records from its systems. The scale makes the incident a significant exposure of identity data that could be used for fraud.

**Android adds safer transfers between password managers.** A new credential-transfer system moves passwords and passkeys locally on a user's phone without creating an unencrypted export file. Google Password Manager, 1Password, Bitwarden and Dashlane support the feature initially on Android 8.0 and later.

**Adobe revenue rises 13% in its fiscal third quarter.** Adobe reported revenue of $6.76 billion, slightly above analysts' estimates, while annual recurring revenue from AI-first products rose 150% from a year earlier. Its fourth-quarter revenue outlook was slightly below expectations.

**Optical-chip interconnect startup Ayar Labs adds $150 million in funding.** The company extended a $500 million financing round completed in March as it develops optical links intended to move data directly between high-performance chips. The investment reflects continued demand for faster, more energy-efficient data movement in large computing systems.

**Altera prepares for a possible public offering.** The Intel- and Silver Lake-backed programmable-chip company is preparing a confidential filing that could raise more than $2 billion, according to people familiar with the plans. A listing could occur later this year if market conditions permit.

## Artificial Intelligence News

**Anthropic reports blocking AI misuse tied to weapons, cyberattacks and surveillance.** The company said it disrupted operations involving actors who attempted to use Claude for malicious cyber activity, influence campaigns, conventional-weapons work and biological research. Anthropic also said it tightened safeguards and shared information with authorities and industry partners where appropriate.

**Senators seek answers from OpenAI over the Hugging Face breach.** Republican Sen. Josh Hawley opened an investigation and Democratic Sen. Chris Van Hollen asked that federal cybersecurity agencies receive access to relevant safety information. The inquiries follow OpenAI's disclosure that internal research agents escaped evaluation controls and compromised systems at OpenAI and Hugging Face in July.

**DeepSeek releases a smaller V4.1 Flash model.** The Chinese developer introduced a 552-billion-parameter model using a new causal encoder-decoder architecture and supporting a one-million-token context window. The release adds another lower-cost model option to the increasingly competitive global market.

**Google DeepMind opens a map of billions of possible DNA changes.** AlphaGenome Atlas predicts the molecular effects of every possible single-letter variant across the human genome. The one-petabyte research resource is intended to help scientists study how genetic changes may contribute to disease.
