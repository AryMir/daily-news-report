## U.S. National News

- **Cost-of-living approval falls in national survey.** An AP-NORC poll found 17% of U.S. adults approve of President Donald Trump's handling of living costs and 26% approve of his handling of the economy. The findings come ahead of the November midterm elections.
- **Weekly unemployment claims decline to 197,000.** The Labor Department reported Thursday that new claims fell from a revised 198,000 the previous week, the lowest level since mid-July. Claims are a widely watched indicator of layoffs.
- **Supreme Court takes immigration detention case.** The justices agreed Thursday to review the administration's policy denying bond hearings to some immigrants held while contesting deportation. Appeals courts have split over whether federal law permits the policy.
- **Senators reach energy permitting agreement.** A bipartisan group announced a bill Wednesday intended to shorten reviews for new energy and infrastructure projects. The proposal also addresses electricity costs associated with AI data centers; it has not become law.
- **Senate delays proposed permanent ban on Chinese vehicles.** Lawmakers failed Wednesday to complete an agreement on legislation that would permanently bar Chinese vehicle sales in the United States. The measure is now expected to wait until after the November elections.

## International News

- **Russian drone strikes Kyiv school.** In Ukraine, a drone hit a school Thursday while children and staff were in shelters. City officials reported damage but no casualties, underscoring the continuing risk to civilians during aerial attacks.
- **North Korea rejects blame for border mine blasts.** In East Asia, Kim Yo Jong denied South Korea's finding that North Korean mines injured three South Korean soldiers near the Demilitarized Zone. Seoul says investigators found another mine nearby; the dispute adds to tensions at the border.
- **Pakistan pledges support for Saudi Arabia.** Pakistan's defense minister said Tuesday that his country would use available means to defend Saudi Arabia against attacks by Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen. The statement raises the possibility of wider involvement in the regional conflict.
- **Somali security forces retake hijacked tanker.** Authorities in Puntland, Somalia, said pirates killed five crew members before forces recovered a fuel tanker off the coast. The attack highlights continuing maritime security risks in the Horn of Africa.
- **Brazil's election nears amid dispute over religious claim.** A claim involving the country's patron saint has become a campaign issue days before the October 4 presidential vote. The son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, a rival of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, denies the allegation.

## Medicare & Medical News for Seniors

- **CMS finalizes a Medicare Part B drug payment test.** Announced September 30, the GLOBE model will test a different rebate calculation for certain clinician-administered drugs in selected areas beginning January 1, 2027. CMS says some affected Original Medicare beneficiaries may see lower out-of-pocket costs from April 2027; that outcome is a goal of the test, not a guaranteed saving for every person.
- **CMS projects a small average increase in stand-alone Part D premiums.** On September 28, CMS estimated the average monthly premium for stand-alone prescription drug plans will rise from $35.09 in 2026 to $36 in 2027. Actual plan costs and covered medicines vary, so beneficiaries can compare 2027 options during open enrollment from October 15 through December 7.
