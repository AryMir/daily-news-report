## U.S. National News

**Administration renews mail-ballot appeal.** The Trump administration asked the Supreme Court to lift a federal block on new mail-ballot restrictions before the midterms. The timing is consequential because North Carolina has begun mailing ballots and other states are scheduled to follow in the coming days.

**August hiring rebounds.** U.S. employers added 162,000 jobs in August, well above the 65,000 expected in a FactSet survey, while unemployment held at 4.1%. Revised June and July figures added 55,000 jobs, shifting attention toward inflation and the Federal Reserve’s next policy decision.

**Midterm campaign enters a volatile final stretch.** Congressional maps have changed in several states, including Texas, California and Florida, while litigation continues over a Missouri map. The unsettled voting rules, economic pressures and foreign-policy developments are shaping a rapidly changing national campaign environment.

**U.S. envoys visit Kyiv after Kremlin meeting.** Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held talks in Kyiv after meeting Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Saturday. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said his government was ready for constructive discussion on a durable peace and security guarantees.

**Washington rejects Iran’s ship-strike claim.** Iran said it struck an unmanned U.S. vessel in the Strait of Hormuz; the U.S. military denied the assertion. The disputed incident followed U.S. strikes on three Iranian oil tankers after attacks on Navy warships, adding to risks for a major energy-shipping route.

## International News

**China’s leader deepens ties with Egypt — Middle East and North Africa.** Xi Jinping met President Abdel Fattah el-Sissi in Cairo, highlighting economic and diplomatic cooperation. The visit comes as China seeks a larger regional role and Egypt looks to diversify strategic partnerships.

**Typhoon Saudel’s rains raise China mudslide toll — East Asia.** The death toll from a mudslide in eastern China rose to three after days of torrential rain associated with Typhoon Saudel. Rescue and recovery efforts continue in affected communities.

**Indonesia closes Jakarta airport during Anak Krakatau eruption — Southeast Asia.** Volcanic ash from Anak Krakatau prompted the cancellation of flights at Jakarta’s Soekarno-Hatta International Airport and disrupted other domestic airports. Authorities are monitoring the eruption and its effect on aviation.

**Greek government announces tax and wage measures amid protests — Europe.** Greece’s government promised tax relief and pay increases before next year’s election as thousands protested high living costs in Thessaloniki. Police estimated more than 25,000 people joined the union-led demonstrations.

## Technology Industry News

**Federal agency opens Tesla Cybercab compliance inquiry.** The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened an audit query into Tesla’s certification of its driverless Cybercab after the vehicle’s commercial deployment in Austin. The review will examine the company’s technical basis for compliance with federal vehicle-safety standards.

**Google issues Chrome update for actively exploited flaw.** Google released Chrome 152.0.7977.82/.83 for Windows and Mac and version .82 for Linux, addressing 12 vulnerabilities. The update includes a high-severity V8 flaw, CVE-2026-85046, that is being exploited in the wild.

**Zoox’s Las Vegas service follows a federal exemption path.** Amazon-owned Zoox received final approval in July for a temporary exemption covering certain federal vehicle-safety standards and has since begun charging for rides in Las Vegas. The authorization limits additions to its commercial fleet to 2,500 vehicles a year for two years.

## Artificial Intelligence News

**Nvidia plans a $12.93 billion purchase of Hugging Face.** Nvidia said it will acquire the open-model platform in a transaction that includes $1 billion for employee retention. The deal would place a widely used hub for open models within the leading supplier of high-end AI chips.

**OpenAI says its Astra model needs stronger safeguards.** OpenAI said an upcoming model demonstrated cybersecurity capabilities that require additional protections under its safety protocol. The company said it has made the model less responsive to harmful cyber requests and will monitor its behavior.

**Justice Department backs OpenAI in copyright case.** The administration filed court papers supporting OpenAI’s argument that training models on large collections of online writing can qualify as fair use. The New York Times and other publishers dispute that position in ongoing litigation.

**U.S. and China prepare for possible AI-safety dialogue.** Officials from the two countries are reportedly preparing for discussions in mid-September focused on frontier-model safety risks. The date, location and participants remain unsettled, and U.S. officials said no meeting had been formally scheduled.
