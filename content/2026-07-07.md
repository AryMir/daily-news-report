## U.S. National News

- Maine Democratic Senate nominee Graham Platner is rapidly losing party support after a former partner accused him of sexual assault in a report released Monday. Platner denied the allegation, but Senate Democratic leaders and other backers are now urging him to leave the race.
- Sen. Mitch McConnell remains hospitalized and is still "continuing his recovery," according to his office, which has not said when the former Republican leader will return to the Capitol. He was admitted on June 14 and has not received a public medical update since last week.
- The U.S. Supreme Court declined to block Texas from enforcing a law that requires app stores to verify users' ages and get parental consent before minors download apps or make in-app purchases. The legal fight now continues while the law stays in effect.
- Former Equal Employment Opportunity Commission member Jocelyn Samuels dropped her lawsuit challenging her dismissal by President Donald Trump. She cited a recent Supreme Court ruling that significantly expanded presidential power over independent agencies.
- New federal data show Affordable Care Act enrollment fell by about 2.6 million people from a year earlier after enhanced subsidies expired in January. Ohio and Oklahoma each lost nearly one-third of their enrollees, according to the state-by-state figures first reported by AP.

## International News

- Hamas said Monday that it had dissolved its government in Gaza and was preparing to transfer authority to a U.N.-backed technical committee as part of a U.S.-brokered ceasefire deal. The group did not say whether it would also disarm or hand over security control.
- A tanker off Oman in the Strait of Hormuz was struck by a projectile and caught fire early Tuesday, according to the British military. Iranian state television said the vessel came under attack after ignoring warnings, though it did not directly claim responsibility.
- Cuba suffered an islandwide blackout on Monday as fuel shortages and the country's aging electrical grid deepened its economic crisis. Officials said protocols were activated to restore service, but the outage hit nearly 10 million people.
- NATO leaders opened their summit in Ankara on Tuesday with U.S. pressure on Europe to spend more on defense and with support for Ukraine still central to the agenda. The meeting comes as Washington signals a smaller direct military role in European security.
- Britain imposed sanctions on Russian institutes and senior staff it said were tied to chemical weapons used against opposition leader Alexei Navalny and former spy Sergei Skripal. The move came ahead of the NATO summit and followed similar action by the European Union.

## Technology Industry News

- Microsoft said it is cutting 4,800 jobs, about 2.1% of its global workforce, with the deepest reductions falling on Xbox. The company said the gaming unit is being reset after weak margins, higher hardware costs, and disappointing growth.
- Broadcom and Apple extended their chip partnership through 2031, keeping Broadcom in place as a key supplier of custom chips for future Apple devices. The deal also reassured investors that Broadcom will remain central to Apple's hardware roadmap.
- A Google executive defended YouTube's decision to keep online a video that falsely claimed a survivor of the Sydney antisemitic massacre was a "crisis actor." The testimony added to scrutiny of how large platforms handle hate content and conspiracy material.
