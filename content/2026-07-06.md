## U.S. National News

- The White House said President Donald Trump plans to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Syrian President Ahmad al-Sharaa during this week’s NATO summit in Turkey, while continuing to press allies on defense-spending targets.
- A White House report released over the Independence Day weekend accused Smithsonian leadership, especially at the National Museum of American History, of pushing divisive activism and signaled potential pressure for leadership changes.
- Super Typhoon Bavi made landfall near Guam over the U.S. territory of Rota with winds above 150 mph and heavy rain, prompting typhoon and flash-flood warnings across the Northern Mariana Islands.
- Two Tennessee National Guard members assigned to a federal anti-crime patrol in Memphis fatally shot a 20-year-old man during a downtown pursuit; the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation is reviewing the shooting.
- A shooting at a July Fourth cookout near New York’s Coney Island beach wounded eight people, including four children, and police said a masked gunman fled after firing into the gathering.

## International News

- Russia launched a major overnight attack on Ukraine with 351 drones and 68 missiles, killing at least 20 people, as Ukrainian officials said shortages of interceptor missiles left ballistic strikes harder to stop.
- Mourners flooded Tehran for the funeral procession of Iran’s late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, with anti-American and anti-Israeli chants dominating the event as wider regional tensions remained unresolved.
- Britain sanctioned nine Russian people and entities for developing chemical weapons that it said were used against opposition leader Alexei Navalny and former spy Sergei Skripal.
- UNICEF said more than 300 children have been killed or injured in Sudan in the first six months of 2026, mostly in drone strikes tied to the country’s continuing civil war.
- More than 1,000 schools in the U.K. were forced to close or send children home early during Europe’s heat wave, highlighting how poorly older public buildings are coping with repeated extreme heat.

The main market theme was rotation rather than broad risk-off selling: investors favored old-economy and defensive shares after the June jobs report, while some AI-linked technology names gave back ground. Oil was lower again Monday morning after OPEC+ raised output and traders continued to price in an easier near-term supply picture.

## Technology Industry News

- Microsoft launched “Microsoft Frontier Company,” a new unit backed by $2.5 billion that will embed roughly 6,000 engineers and specialists inside customer organizations to speed enterprise AI adoption.
- Meta is building a cloud business to sell excess AI computing capacity, a move that would put it into more direct competition with AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud, and specialist AI infrastructure providers.
- Xbox said worldwide console price increases will take effect on August 1, with U.S. prices rising by $100 for 512 GB models and $150 for 1 TB models; the company is also sunsetting its 2 TB console model.
- An AP/FRONTLINE investigation found that U.S. AI tools, internet providers, and connectivity services are being used to industrialize scam compounds in Southeast Asia, widening concern about tech-sector accountability and enforcement.

## Artificial Intelligence News

- A senior U.K. Financial Conduct Authority official said Britain should consider whether general-purpose AI models such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini need direct regulation rather than relying only on sector-by-sector rules.
- Reuters reported that the U.S. government is in advanced talks with AI companies on voluntary standards for releasing new frontier models, including staged access and review mechanisms.
- OpenAI said it delayed the full public rollout of GPT-5.6 at the U.S. government’s request, limiting initial access to a smaller group of vetted partners.
- Heat-wave pressure on data centers is intensifying political and neighborhood opposition to AI infrastructure, with AP reporting new complaints in Massachusetts over power demand, water use, diesel backup generators, and local air quality.
