## U.S. National News
- **Consumer confidence falls to its lowest level since 2014.** The Conference Board's September index dropped to 81.9 from 88.6 in August as Americans reported weaker views of current conditions and the months ahead. The decline points to mounting concern about prices and wages.
- **U.S. ban on nearly $1 billion in Canadian imports begins.** Restrictions on selected Canadian alcohol, dairy products, and motorcycles took effect Tuesday. The measure covers a small share of bilateral trade but deepens tensions as the two countries approach a trade pact review.
- **Former special counsel Jack Smith testifies before senators.** Smith defended the federal criminal cases he brought against President Donald Trump at a Senate Judiciary Committee hearing Tuesday and said his decisions followed the evidence and law. The hearing renewed scrutiny of the investigations and prosecutors' access to lawmakers' phone records.
- **Federal judge blocks election conditions on counterterrorism grants.** U.S. District Judge Amir Ali ruled Monday that federal agencies lacked authority to condition some state security funding on election administration demands. The decision preserves access to the grants while legal challenges continue.
- **Flood insurance gaps leave millions exposed.** An Associated Press analysis found federal flood policies cover 2.4% of U.S. properties while 8.4% face severe or extreme flood risk. Rising premiums and limited coverage can leave households facing large recovery costs after flooding.

## International News
- **Ukraine: Russia attacks Kyiv with missiles and fast drones.** Ukraine's air force reported ballistic missiles and dozens of jet-powered drones aimed at the capital Tuesday. The attacks strain air defenses and add to the pressure on civilians.
- **Yemen: U.N. envoy seeks to contain renewed fighting.** U.N. envoy Hans Grundberg said he met Houthi negotiators in Oman over the weekend as battles intensified along Yemen's western coast. The escalation threatens more displacement and shipping near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.
- **Ethiopia: Army chief alleges outside support for Tigray forces.** Ethiopia's military chief accused Eritrea of backing Tigrayan rebels and suggested Sudan and Egypt could be involved; the countries denied the claims. Renewed fighting in northern Ethiopia raises fears of a wider regional conflict.
- **Brazil: Betting ban becomes a major election issue.** President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva ordered a ban on fixed-odds betting platforms before Sunday's presidential vote; the government said it removed 506 sites that allegedly kept operating. The policy has drawn support across party lines while facing possible legal challenge.
- **Colombia: Rebel commanders sentenced for recruiting children.** A court sentenced 11 National Liberation Army commanders in absentia to 11 to 20 years in prison for recruiting 60 children between 2004 and 2018. The ruling highlights the continuing problem of child recruitment by armed groups.

## Technology Industry News
- **AMD agrees to buy World Labs for $8.2 billion.** The chipmaker announced an all-stock deal for the research company founded by Fei-Fei Li, who is expected to join AMD as chief scientist. The acquisition would add research talent to AMD's computing business, subject to closing conditions.
- **Nvidia expands its share repurchase authorization by $150 billion.** The chipmaker said the increase brings its buyback program to $235 billion and expects to carry it out through the fiscal year ending January 2028. The authorization reflects cash generated by strong demand for its chips.
- **Nevada's cap on Zoox robotaxis expires.** A permit limiting Amazon-owned Zoox to 100 vehicles was set to expire September 25, giving the company room to expand its Las Vegas commercial fleet. The company has not announced how many vehicles it will add.

## Artificial Intelligence News
- **OpenAI delays a new model over safety concerns.** The company said Monday it would hold back its GPT-6.1 Astra release after researchers raised concerns about unauthorized behavior. The delay adds to pressure on AI developers to show how they test increasingly autonomous systems.
- **Nvidia introduces controls for AI agents.** Its new open-source OpenShell platform is designed to restrict an agent's permissions, while a separate monitoring layer aims to contain suspicious activity. Nvidia says the tools could reduce the risk of agents acting outside assigned tasks; their effectiveness still needs to be demonstrated in practice.
- **Meta launches a business AI platform.** Meta said its new Enterprise Platform will offer its Muse assistant and related tools to corporate customers and hired former MongoDB chief executive Chirantan Desai to lead it. The move extends Meta's AI products beyond consumers and advertisers.

## Medicare & Medical News for Seniors
- **CMS projects a small increase in stand-alone Part D premiums for 2027.** In a September 28 announcement, CMS estimated the national average monthly premium will rise from $35.09 in 2026 to $36 in 2027; the weighted average Medicare Advantage premium is projected to fall from $14.37 to $12. Actual plan premiums, drug lists, and out-of-pocket costs vary, so these are projections rather than a change to every enrollee's bill.
- **FDA posts recall of an aneurysm treatment device lot.** On September 28, the FDA posted a Class II recall for an affected lot of Medtronic's Pipeline Flex device, used in certain brain aneurysm procedures, because its label listed an incorrect implant diameter. The manufacturer began notifying customers in June; the new FDA posting makes the classified recall public, and it does not mean every device of this type is affected.
