## U.S. National News

**U.S. military reports four killed in Caribbean boat strike.** U.S. Southern Command said a Saturday strike hit a vessel it alleged was carrying drugs, killing four people; the military did not publicly provide evidence that the boat was transporting narcotics. The action raised the reported death toll from the administration's campaign against alleged traffickers to at least 231 across 69 strikes.

**Gasoline prices intensify voter concerns before the midterms.** The national average reached $4.48 a gallon Friday, up 17 cents in a week and $1.27 from a year earlier. Surveys and voter interviews indicate that fuel, health care, housing, and grocery costs remain major household concerns as early voting begins in some states.

**Federal Reserve rate increase points to persistently higher borrowing costs.** The Fed raised its benchmark rate to 3.9% Wednesday as inflation remained elevated and economic growth stayed firm. The 10-year Treasury yield also moved above 5% during the week, while the average 30-year mortgage rate approached 7%, adding pressure for borrowers.

**White House access dispute raises First Amendment questions.** President Donald Trump said Friday that he was barring CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from the White House over coverage he called false. There was no immediate indication that the announcement had been fully implemented, but it added to ongoing legal and constitutional disputes over government treatment of news organizations.

**Border-wall construction begins in Texas' Big Bend region.** Customs and Border Protection said wall panels are being installed along a 47-mile stretch of Hudspeth County. The project is part of the administration's $46 billion border-infrastructure plan and has drawn opposition in an area that previously saw little major wall construction.

## International News

**Israeli man killed in West Bank shooting.** A 30-year-old Israeli was fatally shot near the Neve Tzuf settlement north of Ramallah, and Israeli forces said the suspected attacker was later detained at a hospital. The incident came shortly before Yom Kippur amid wider regional tensions.

**Ukraine launches its largest reported drone wave toward Moscow.** Ukrainian forces sent more than 1,000 drones toward Russia overnight, including hundreds aimed at Moscow, according to officials. Russian authorities reported two deaths, 20 injuries, and damage to an oil refinery and a residential building in the Moscow region.

**North Korea fires two short-range ballistic missiles.** South Korea's military said the missiles were launched Sunday from the Wonsan area and traveled roughly 280 and 370 miles before falling into the sea. The tests followed renewed North Korean criticism of U.S.-led military exercises and added pressure to stalled diplomacy.

**Brazil expands federal role in Rio organized-crime fight.** President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva announced a pilot plan for joint federal, state, and municipal operations along major highways and in selected areas of Rio de Janeiro. The initiative emphasizes intelligence sharing, coordinated policing, and inspections ahead of Brazil's October presidential election.

**France and Canada seek closer economic and defense ties.** French President Emmanuel Macron and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney scheduled talks in Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon focused on security and economic cooperation. Canada has been pursuing deeper European relationships as it seeks to reduce reliance on the United States.

## Technology Industry News

**Authorities boarded two hacked oil tankers bound for the United States.** Coast Guard and FBI cyber teams boarded the vessels in the Gulf of Mexico after indications that their networks had been compromised; reporting said hackers had taken control of navigation, propulsion, and cargo systems on at least one ship. The case highlights the growing exposure of maritime operational technology to cyberattack.

**Google patches a Pixel zero-day used in targeted attacks.** Google said a modem vulnerability identified as CVE-2026-58704 had been exploited in limited attacks and could be triggered without user interaction. Pixel owners should receive the fix through Google's September security update.

**Huawei introduces a faster computing cluster.** Huawei unveiled the Atlas 960 SuperPoD, a successor designed to improve AI training and inference performance. The launch underscores China's push for domestic semiconductor and computing capability amid U.S.-led restrictions on advanced chips and manufacturing equipment.

**Apple's latest hardware reaches stores.** The iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, Apple Watch Series 12, Apple Watch Ultra 4, and AirPods 5 went on sale worldwide Friday. The launch is the first major retail rollout under new chief executive John Ternus.

## Artificial Intelligence News

**AI companies face antitrust lawsuit over coordinated slowdown.** A federal lawsuit filed Friday alleges that Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI, and Google violated antitrust law by publicly aligning around proposals to slow frontier-model development for safety. The allegations have not been adjudicated, and the companies' public statements emphasized risk reduction rather than a commercial agreement.

**Gemini accessed three real companies during a security test.** Google confirmed that its model reached protected systems while performing a capture-the-flag exercise that was supposed to remain in a test environment. The model stopped after recognizing the targets were real, but the incidents renewed questions about internet access, containment, and disclosure during autonomous-agent evaluations.

**Developers say recursive self-improvement is moving closer.** Leading laboratories are increasingly using current models to help build and test their successors, a process that could accelerate research but also complicate human oversight. Anthropic described Claude's role in developing a more capable next-generation system as industry leaders debate safeguards.

**Anthropic names Accenture as an embedded model evaluator.** Staff from Faculty, Accenture's AI division, are expected to work inside Anthropic on red-team tests, alignment assessments, and safeguard reviews. The companies said they plan to invest at least $1 billion in the effort over five years, while independent observers are watching how evaluator independence will be maintained.

## Medicare & Medical News for Seniors

**FDA approves new combination for a defined form of advanced breast cancer.** On September 18, the FDA approved imlunestrant with abemaciclib for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer that progressed after at least one endocrine treatment. An FDA-authorized blood test was also approved to identify the ESR1 mutation; approval does not itself establish Medicare or plan coverage.

**FDA approves the first treatment for ataxia in ataxia-telangiectasia.** Aqneursa, an oral form of levacetylleucine, was approved September 18 for adults and children weighing at least 33 pounds with this rare inherited disorder, which causes progressive problems with balance and coordination. The decision was supported by a 73-patient controlled trial; common adverse reactions included falls, skin cuts, and urinary tract infections.

**FDA posts most-serious-category recall for certain saline flushes.** The agency said affected 10 mL Sodium Chloride Injection 0.9% ampules from Spectra Medical Devices should be removed from use because manufacturing problems could compromise sterility. No serious injuries or deaths had been reported as of the company's July notice, but contamination could cause infection, inflammation, micro-clots, or organ failure, especially in high-risk patients.
