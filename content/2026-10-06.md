## U.S. National News

- **Arizona court breach exposes personal records.** Court officials said a September 24 cyberattack copied records for 1.3 million people with unpaid court obligations, along with other sensitive records. Officials said court cases have not been delayed and affected people are being notified.
- **Alito describes pressure from emergency appeals.** In an interview published today, Supreme Court Justice Samuel Alito said the growing emergency docket is troubling but difficult for the court to control. He also said harsh political rhetoric contributes to declining public trust.

## International News

- **Yemen: fighting intensifies near a shipping chokepoint.** Saudi Arabia and Yemen’s government are trying to dislodge Houthi forces from the Bab el-Mandeb area. The Houthis have responded with missile and drone attacks, adding uncertainty along a major Red Sea trade route.
- **Ukraine and Russia: another large overnight drone exchange.** Russia’s Defense Ministry said it intercepted nearly 900 Ukrainian drones. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said intelligence indicated preparations for a major Russian aerial attack and that countermeasures were underway.
- **Canada: Quebec’s election revives the independence debate.** Prime Minister Mark Carney urged attention to housing, jobs and economic growth after the separatist Parti Québécois won Monday’s election. The incoming provincial leader has pledged another independence referendum, with timing still unsettled.
- **Southeast Asia: rising electricity demand strains energy security.** A new International Energy Agency report warns that demand is outpacing countries’ responses to supply risks. It recommends more cross-border electricity sharing and a broader mix of power sources.
- **Serbia: overseas voting rules draw criticism.** The election commission tied voting abroad to Serbia’s own polling hours for the October 25 parliamentary election. Critics say the resulting overnight hours in some countries could make participation harder.
