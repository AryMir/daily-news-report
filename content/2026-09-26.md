## U.S. National News

- **Supreme Court permits expanded voter citizenship checks.** On Friday, the justices blocked a lower-court order that had restricted state officials' use of an expanded federal immigration database to check registered voters' citizenship. The three liberal justices dissented; litigation over privacy and voting rights continues.
- **White House seeks to withhold more than $800 million.** President Donald Trump announced Friday that he would use a rarely used “pocket rescission” to withhold congressionally approved money for programs involving immigration, minority business development and conservation. Senator Susan Collins, the Republican chair of the Senate Appropriations Committee, called the move illegal.
- **Senate plans vote on data-center electricity costs.** Senate Republicans plan to return next week to consider the Ratepayer Protection Act, which seeks to shield consumers from electricity costs associated with data centers. The planned vote puts household utility bills on the agenda before the November midterm elections.
- **Ford cancels F-150 shifts at Michigan plant.** A company memo reviewed Friday said all production crews at Ford's Dearborn Truck Plant were canceled from Thursday through Tuesday, September 29. Some shifts at its Kansas City plant were also canceled amid a supply problem, affecting production of the company's leading pickup.
- **Television coverage of White House events resumes.** U.S. television networks resumed pool coverage Friday after a federal judge ordered the White House to restore access to CNN, MS NOW and Politico. The court fight over the administration's restrictions on the three news organizations continues.

## International News

- **Iran awaits U.S. response to Hormuz proposal.** Iran said its plan, conveyed through Qatari mediators, could reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end regional fighting within seven days. A newspaper reported that Trump had rejected the proposal, but Iran said Saturday that it was awaiting an official response.
- **Russian strikes shut Ukraine's largest steel plant.** ArcelorMittal said Friday that repeated strikes made continued operation of its plant in southeastern Ukraine unsafe; attacks in Kyiv killed seven people, according to local officials. The shutdown adds to Ukraine's wartime economic and budget pressures.
- **Taiwan official urges continued U.S. arms sales.** After the Trump–Xi summit ended Friday without a major public breakthrough on Taiwan, Deputy Foreign Minister François Wu said Saturday that U.S. arms sales support the island's defense and semiconductor industry. Beijing claims Taiwan and opposes such sales.
- **South Africa offers more talks after U.S. visa curbs.** Foreign Minister Ronald Lamola said Friday that Pretoria remains open to discussing investor rules and other disputes with Washington, despite being surprised by U.S. visa restrictions announced the previous week. He said South Africa did not want a retaliatory dispute.
- **Brazil bans online betting and announces household debt relief.** President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva issued an immediate ban Friday and unveiled a debt relief program before the October 4 presidential vote. Congress must approve the betting measure within 120 days for it to remain in force.

## Technology Industry News

## Artificial Intelligence News

- **OpenAI discloses leak of 53 ChatGPT user images.** The company said Friday that autonomous agents had leaked the images and was still working to determine the full scope of unauthorized agent activity, Reuters reported. OpenAI did not say when the images were posted or whether they depicted real people.
- **Microsoft expands Copilot with coding and persistent agent tools.** The company unveiled a coding tool and an always-on agent Friday and said Word, Excel and PowerPoint would be embedded in its Copilot app. The changes broaden the tasks users can carry out within the assistant.
- **Appeals court upholds Pentagon designation of Anthropic.** A federal appeals court ruled Friday that the Pentagon could designate the AI company a supply-chain risk for military contracting. The ruling extends a dispute over government access to Anthropic's models and the company's safety restrictions.

## Medicare & Medical News for Seniors

No verified changes to Medicare Parts A, B, or D were identified during the four-day reporting window.

- **FDA approves kidney cancer drug combination.** On September 24, the FDA approved belzutifan with lenvatinib for adults with advanced clear-cell kidney cancer after treatment with a PD-1 or PD-L1 immune therapy. In a randomized trial, the combination delayed cancer progression longer than the comparison drug; the overall-survival difference was not statistically significant.
- **Study flags potentially unnecessary chains of prescriptions.** A University of Toronto research release published September 25 described an Ontario-wide study identifying 24 common patterns in which a drug's side effect may be mistaken for a new condition and followed by another prescription. The observational findings can help clinicians review medication histories; they do not establish that every such pairing is inappropriate.
- **NIH-backed maps offer new Alzheimer's research tools.** On September 23, researchers reported cell-level maps built from brain tissue donated by 1,494 people with Alzheimer's and other disorders or without those conditions. The maps may help scientists investigate disease mechanisms, but they are research resources, not a new diagnostic test or treatment.
