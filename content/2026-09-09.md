## U.S. National News

**U.S. announces new restrictions on selected Canadian imports.** The White House said the United States will bar some Canadian dairy products and motorcycles, along with most alcoholic beverages, beginning September 29. The action follows Canadian retaliatory tariffs on $20 billion in U.S. goods and raises the prospect of further disruption for cross-border businesses and consumers.

**Republicans convene a Dallas midterm convention.** Republican officials and candidates are gathering in Dallas for a two-day convention centered on President Donald Trump and the 2026 midterm elections. The event is unusual because national party conventions are generally held for presidential nominations, rather than congressional campaigns.

**Investigators examine attempted go-around before Miami cargo crash.** Flight data indicate that an Amazon cargo jet attempted to abort an unstable landing before overrunning a Miami International Airport runway Sunday, killing five people in a van. The National Transportation Safety Board is reviewing flight and cockpit-recording data; its final cause finding is expected to take much longer.

**Missouri congressional-map litigation leaves election rules uncertain.** A federal judge temporarily blocked use of Missouri's old congressional districts on Tuesday, shortly after the U.S. Supreme Court left in place a state-court order directing the state to use them. The conflicting orders come eight weeks before Election Day and leave some voters and candidates uncertain about their districts.

**Smithsonian Secretary Lonnie Bunch says he will retire.** Bunch, who has led the Smithsonian Institution since 2019, announced he will leave at the end of the year. His departure comes amid a public dispute with the White House over museum presentations of American history.

## International News

**Iran and the United States trade attacks on shipping near the Strait of Hormuz.** Iran said it struck 10 ships near the waterway after U.S. forces destroyed five Iranian oil tankers, in the largest declared exchange against shipping since the conflict began. The attacks add risks to energy transport through a route that previously carried about one-fifth of global oil supply.

**Deadly explosion reported at a weapons depot in Syria.** Syrian state television said at least 14 people were killed and 11 wounded when a temporary weapons-and-war-remnants depot exploded near Sarmada in Idlib. The cause was not immediately reported.

**Russian drone strike kills two at Ukraine-Moldova border crossing.** Ukrainian and Moldovan officials said drones hit the Starokozache crossing in Odesa, killing two civilians and injuring three. Passenger and vehicle traffic was halted, highlighting growing pressure on Ukraine's land logistics routes.

**Serbia calls an October snap parliamentary election.** President Aleksandar Vucic dissolved parliament and set an October 25 vote, after months of anti-corruption protests. Vucic said he plans to run for prime minister, extending a political crisis that began after the 2024 Novi Sad station collapse.

**Brazil's top court orders federal police chief reinstated.** Supreme Court Justice Flavio Dino ordered Andrei Rodrigues returned to his role one day after another justice suspended him. The ruling is part of a dispute within Brazil's top court and police leadership ahead of next month's presidential election.

## Technology Industry News

**Analog Devices agrees to buy Alif Semiconductor for $1.35 billion.** The chipmaker said it will acquire Alif in cash, with possible contingent payments of up to $200 million. The deal adds low-power processors used in consumer and industrial equipment, expanding Analog Devices' capabilities for systems that process data close to sensors.

**Microsoft issues a record-sized September security update.** Microsoft's Patch Tuesday release addresses 966 vulnerabilities, including two zero-days reported as actively exploited. Organizations and individual users should prioritize the security updates, particularly for systems exposed to untrusted files, email, or remote access.

**Google patches an actively exploited Chrome vulnerability.** Google released Chrome updates addressing 230 flaws, including CVE-2026-87491, which it says has an exploit in the wild. The company is rolling out updated versions for Windows, macOS, and Linux; users should restart Chrome after the update is installed.

## Artificial Intelligence News

**China rejects U.S. accusations involving AI-model extraction.** U.S. cyber and intelligence agencies said Chinese developers had extracted capabilities from leading U.S. AI models through large-scale distillation activity. China's foreign ministry called the accusations unfounded; AI governance is expected to be part of planned U.S.-China discussions.

**Meta introduces Muse, a personal AI agent for U.S. adults.** Meta said Muse can assist with tasks such as scheduling, shopping, email, and travel booking. The company said the agent runs in a dedicated virtual machine and is initially available in the United States, with privacy and approval controls emphasized in the rollout.

**Anthropic reports a computer-checked formalization of Fermat's Last Theorem.** The company said Claude worked largely autonomously over 11 days to produce a Lean-language formalization, which it says was computer checked. The work formalizes an established theorem rather than replacing the earlier human proof, but illustrates AI's expanding role in mathematical verification.

**Seattle Times and Newsday sue OpenAI and Microsoft.** The newspapers allege the companies used their journalism without permission to train AI products and seek damages and the destruction of relevant training datasets and models. Microsoft said it was surprised by the lawsuit and was open to discussing solutions.

**Reported U.S.-China AI-safety talks remain unconfirmed.** Reuters reported that the two countries were preparing for a mid-September dialogue on AI safety risks, while a White House official said no AI-related meeting was currently planned. The differing accounts leave the timing and scope of any talks unsettled.
