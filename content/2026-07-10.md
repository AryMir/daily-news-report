## U.S. National News

- A Houston ICE shooting is under scrutiny after AP reported that Lorenzo Salgado Araujo, a Mexican national who had lived in the U.S. for decades, was not the target of the operation, according to Rep. Sylvia Garcia. The case has drawn added criticism because the ICE agents involved were not wearing body cameras.
- Federal prosecutors indicted eight men in Ohio on murder and terrorism conspiracy charges tied to an alleged drone-and-sniper attack plot against the White House's June UFC event. AP reported the group allegedly targeted President Donald Trump, Vice President JD Vance, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, Elon Musk, and others.
- A federal appeals court upheld Illinois' ban on semiautomatic weapons, reversing a lower-court ruling and finding the law consistent with the nation's historical tradition of firearm regulation. The law bars AR-15-style rifles, large-capacity magazines, and certain attachments.
- Meteorologists told AP that a large and prolonged heat dome is expected to spread dangerous temperatures across much of the continental United States starting this weekend. Forecasts call for temperatures 15 to 25 degrees above normal in many areas, with unusually hot nights raising the health risk.
- President Trump removed two Democratic members of the bipartisan Election Assistance Commission, further reshaping the federal voting oversight body after a recent Supreme Court ruling strengthened presidential removal power over independent agencies. The move could complicate election administration ahead of the midterms.

## International News

- Unclaimed airstrikes hit southern Iran after the U.S. said it had ended its latest offensive, adding new uncertainty to an already fragile regional situation. AP said the strikes raised immediate questions about who else may now be targeting the Islamic Republic.
- A wildfire in Almeria, Spain, killed at least 12 people and left 23 others missing, making it one of the country's deadliest fires on record. AP reported the blaze spread quickly through extreme heat, strong winds, and drought conditions.
- Typhoon Bavi is tracking toward China's east coast after forcing school closures in Taipei and prompting evacuations and ferry suspensions. AP said 17,000 people were evacuated in Zhejiang and 170,000 rescue workers were placed on standby.
- The Philippines marked the 10th anniversary of the 2016 South China Sea arbitration ruling that rejected Beijing's sweeping maritime claims. China continues to reject the ruling, leaving the dispute one of Asia's most persistent flashpoints.
- A fire at a shoe factory in Jinjiang, China, killed 28 people in one of the country's deadliest industrial blazes in recent years. Authorities detained the factory owner and other responsible parties while investigators examine the cause.

## Technology Industry News

- SK Hynix began trading on Nasdaq after pricing a $26.5 billion U.S. listing, the biggest foreign IPO in the U.S. according to AP. The debut underscores how strongly investors are still backing memory-chip suppliers tied to AI infrastructure demand.
- The European Union accused Meta of using addictive design features on Facebook and Instagram, including infinite scrolling and autoplay, and demanded changes under the Digital Services Act. A final ruling could bring fines of up to 6% of Meta's global annual revenue.
- Senior British security officials warned that technology platforms are making it easier for hostile states, extremists, and far-right actors to spread threats and radicalize users online. AP said officials specifically pointed to growing pressure from Iran, Russia, and China, along with youth radicalization on digital platforms.

## Artificial Intelligence News

- Major news organizations asked a federal judge to sanction OpenAI in a copyright case over AI training data, alleging discovery misconduct and destruction of evidence. The case remains one of the most closely watched legal fights over how AI companies use copyrighted material.
- AI notetaking tools are gaining wider use, but AP reported that lawyers, privacy experts, and other professionals are warning about consent, data retention, biometric voiceprint collection, and privilege concerns. The biggest concern is that sensitive meetings can quietly become training or storage data.
- Meta said it will invest about $9.1 billion to build its first AI data center in Canada, in Alberta, and its largest such facility outside the United States. The project highlights the scale of infrastructure spending still flowing into AI even as environmental and grid concerns grow.
- The Trump administration lifted restrictions on Anthropic's latest Claude models after a weekslong cybersecurity-related ban, restoring broader use while leaving the larger federal scrutiny of frontier AI systems in place.
