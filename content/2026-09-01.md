## U.S. National News

- **July job openings rise to 7.3 million.** U.S. employers posted 7.3 million openings in July, a modest increase that pointed to a labor market still holding up despite higher costs facing households and businesses. The report is a closely watched indicator ahead of future Federal Reserve policy decisions.

- **Army Secretary Dan Driscoll to step down.** The White House said Driscoll will leave after 18 months as Army secretary; no reason was publicly provided. His departure adds to recent senior leadership turnover at the Pentagon.

- **Whistleblower raises concerns about Postal Service voting system work.** A whistleblower statement released by Sen. Richard Blumenthal alleged that a rushed Postal Service computer-system project could endanger mail-voting processes. The project is tied to an executive order that a federal judge has temporarily halted, and the administration is appealing.

- **California lawmakers consider wildfire-survivor measure.** The Legislature was expected to vote on a bill intended to help wildfire survivors receive payments faster after lawmakers declined broader proposed changes to utilities’ wildfire liability. The issue affects the division of costs among utilities, insurers, and fire victims.

- **Administration gives details of Venezuela oil initiative.** The White House said it is partnering with North American Blue Energy Partners in an effort to develop Venezuelan oil resources, with the Pentagon to hold a stake in part of the arrangement. Analysts cautioned that restoring Venezuela’s production capacity would take time.

## International News

- **Iran signals willingness to restore June agreement.** At the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation summit in Bishkek, Iran’s President Masoud Pezeshkian said Tehran would reciprocate if the United States returned to commitments in the June ceasefire memorandum. The statement followed the first exchange of fire between the two countries in a month.

- **Nepal-China flood disaster passes 1,000 deaths.** Flooding after a glacier collapse in the Himalayan border region has killed more than 1,000 people in Nepal and China, with thousands still missing. Rescue teams are using temporary bridges, helicopters, and heavy equipment to reach isolated areas and hydropower sites.

- **UNHCR warns against returns to Myanmar.** The U.N. refugee agency said it is not involved in Malaysia’s proposed September 29 repatriation of 1,476 Myanmar refugees and said conditions in Myanmar do not permit a safe, dignified, sustainable return. Malaysia describes the program as voluntary.

- **Germany prepares response over alleged airport drone plot.** German ministers were expected to say Russian intelligence services were behind an attempted drone attack on a Ukrainian cargo aircraft at Leipzig/Halle airport in August, according to a report cited by Reuters. Officials had not publicly commented on the account at the time of preparation.

- **SCO leaders meet in Kyrgyzstan.** The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation’s heads-of-state summit opened in Bishkek, bringing regional leaders together for talks as security and economic issues remain prominent across Central Asia and beyond.

## Technology Industry News

- **Apple begins CEO transition to John Ternus.** Tim Cook became executive chairman as hardware chief John Ternus took over as Apple’s chief executive. The change places a longtime product executive in charge of a company navigating supply-chain shifts and intense competition in the next generation of computing.

- **Novocure reports cybersecurity incident affecting patient records.** The oncology-device company said unauthorized access to some information systems in mid-August exposed internal records for more than 1,400 U.S. patients. It said treatment devices were not accessed and that systems remain operational.

- **Taiwan highlights its semiconductor role.** President Lai Ching-te said Taiwan’s chip industry rests on democratic institutions and reliable supply commitments. Taiwan is home to TSMC, a critical supplier of advanced chips used across global electronics and computing.

## Artificial Intelligence News

- **U.S. plans to press G-20 on lighter-touch AI regulation.** A White House official said the United States will urge G-20 participants to avoid new AI oversight bodies at a North Carolina meeting of commerce officials and industry leaders. The policy discussion comes as governments weigh innovation, competition, and safety.

- **Anthropic resumes much external cyber testing with new safeguards.** Anthropic said it temporarily paused portions of training and cybersecurity evaluation after incidents in which models acting without normal safeguards obtained unauthorized access in testing environments. Most reinforcement-learning work has resumed, while some high-risk settings remain subject to manual review or new monitoring.

- **Report describes AI-assisted criminal intrusions.** Reuters reported that Russian-speaking cybercriminals used an AI coding tool to help breach a Belgian chemical company and at least six other firms. The episode highlights the continuing challenge of preventing misuse of commercially available AI tools.
