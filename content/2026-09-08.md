## U.S. National News

- **Senate control has become a competitive midterm issue.** With less than two months before Election Day, both parties are treating control of the Senate as unsettled; total spending across Senate contests is expected to exceed $3.4 billion. The outcome could shape the administration's ability to confirm nominees and judges.

- **Food-safety scrutiny follows a cyclospora outbreak tied to lettuce.** A nationwide outbreak connected to contaminated lettuce has renewed attention on imported-produce safeguards, as federal regulators have conducted fewer foreign inspections in recent years.

- **Hurricane Lowell disrupts Hawaii.** Damaging winds and heavy rain reached the western Hawaiian Islands, with Kauai and Niihau under a hurricane warning and Oahu under a tropical-storm warning. Schools and government offices were closed on the three islands as flooding, landslide, and surf risks continued.

- **U.S. forces sank an Ecuadorian vessel alleged to support drug trafficking.** U.S. Southern Command said it intercepted and sank a floating refueling station in the eastern Pacific that it linked to the Los Choneros criminal group; people aboard were removed for transfer to Ecuadorian authorities.

- **States vary widely in how they teach the Sept. 11 attacks.** At least 40 states expect or suggest public schools address Sept. 11, but requirements differ substantially in detail and framing as the 25th anniversary approaches.

## International News

- **Canada begins retaliatory tariffs on U.S. goods.** Canada imposed tariffs on about $20 billion in American goods in response to the latest U.S. measures, escalating a dispute with direct consequences for North American businesses and supply chains.

- **Ukraine sees no immediate breakthrough after U.S.-brokered talks.** President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said visiting U.S. envoys offered worthwhile ideas for ending Russia's war, but fighting resumed after the diplomatic contacts and Moscow maintained its military objectives.

- **Houthi strikes hit Saudi energy facilities.** Attacks from Yemen wounded 73 people and caused fires at oil facilities and utilities in southern Saudi Arabia, prompting temporary operational suspensions and intensifying worries over regional energy supplies.

- **Jakarta-area airports resume service after volcanic ash closures.** Airports serving Indonesia's capital and nearby regions reopened after ash from an Anak Krakatau eruption forced a two-day shutdown that halted nearly 3,000 flights.

- **China reports strong August trade growth.** Customs data showed exports rose 25% from a year earlier and imports climbed 28.2%, widening the monthly trade surplus to about $119 billion. The figures underscore the importance of external demand to China's economy.

## Technology Industry News

- **ASML and major chipmakers map out the next lithography transition.** ASML is working with leading customers on its High-NA technology and larger photomasks; TSMC targets advanced-node volume production from 2030 and Samsung plans High-NA EUV use in DRAM production from 2028.

- **Apple prepares a foldable iPhone launch under new leadership.** Analysts expect Apple to show a premium folding phone at its Cupertino event, potentially priced above $2,500. The launch is a test of whether a late entrant can build a significant position in a still-small foldables market.

- **Digital-infrastructure dependence moves onto board agendas.** A survey of 1,300 large organizations in 11 countries found growing concern about dependence on critical data, cloud, and telecommunications providers amid cyber threats, export controls, and geopolitical disruption.

- **Australia proposes greater user control over social-media feeds.** The government's proposed “My Feed, My Way” initiative would require platforms to notify users and let them choose whether to receive algorithmically curated content.
