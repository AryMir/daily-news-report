## U.S. National News

- **Warsh stresses inflation fight in Jackson Hole address.** Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh said Friday that inflation has not shown enough improvement to relax the central bank's focus, putting the next policy meeting in mid-September under closer scrutiny. The upcoming inflation report may heavily influence whether policymakers raise rates.

- **Administration appeals ruling that blocked mail-voting order.** The Trump administration appealed a court decision that prevented limits on mail voting from taking effect before the midterm elections. The case continues a legal dispute over how much authority the federal government has over election procedures that are largely administered by states.

- **Colorado River states face planned water reductions.** A federal plan would require California, Nevada and Arizona collectively to cut annual Colorado River use by 1.25 million acre-feet over the next two years, with potentially larger reductions if conditions worsen. The river serves about 40 million people and extensive Western agriculture.

- **Two wildfires challenge crews along California's Big Sur coast.** Firefighters worked on a new Plaskett Fire that prompted evacuation orders near Highway 1 while the larger Timber Fire, active since Aug. 8, remained only partly contained. High fire danger persists amid lightning risk and gusty winds.

- **Judge vacates broad immigration-visa suspension.** A federal judge in New York set aside a State Department policy that suspended immigrant-visa processing for nationals of 75 countries, finding it exceeded the secretary of state's authority. The ruling is another court challenge to the administration's immigration agenda.

## International News

- **Russia's strike near Kyiv kills at least 37 people.** Ukraine said a Russian attack near the capital killed at least 37 people Saturday, making it one of the year's deadliest strikes in the country. The attack adds to continued pressure on Ukraine's air defenses and civilian infrastructure.

- **Nepal-China border disaster leaves hundreds dead and thousands missing.** Rescue efforts continued after flash floods linked to a glacial collapse devastated Himalayan communities; officials reported nearly 600 deaths and almost 2,500 people missing. Authorities also warned that a newly formed high-altitude lake could create further flooding risk.

- **Former Ecuadorian president Lenín Moreno receives corruption sentence.** A court sentenced Moreno to five years in prison after finding him guilty of accepting bribes connected to a hydroelectric contract during his time as vice president. Because of a disability, he is to serve the sentence under house arrest.

- **South Africa investigates mass sardine deaths.** Scientists are examining whether pilchard herpesvirus contributed to large numbers of sardines washing ashore along South Africa's coasts. The event has affected a seawater intake at one unit of the Koeberg nuclear power station; officials say the virus poses no known human-health risk.

- **Iranian leadership consolidates after six months of war.** Reporting from the region describes Iranian military and clerical figures tightening control as the conflict with the United States and Israel reaches six months. The prolonged war continues to affect regional security and global energy routes.

## Technology Industry News

- **Nvidia reports revenue of $96.22 billion and a stronger outlook.** The chipmaker's fiscal second-quarter revenue more than doubled from a year earlier and exceeded analyst expectations, while adjusted earnings per share also beat forecasts. - **ATF confirms ransomware-related breach of a standalone system.** The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives said a ransomware group accessed a system containing information on investigation targets. - **EU AI Act moves into a new enforcement phase.** Transparency requirements that took effect in August are beginning to shape how companies operating in Europe document and label certain AI outputs.
