## U.S. National News

- **August payroll growth exceeded expectations.** U.S. employers added 162,000 jobs in August and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%; food services, local-government education and manufacturing added jobs, while information employment fell. The report sharpened attention on the Federal Reserve’s September meeting.

- **Federal judge blocks mail-voting directive again.** U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani extended an injunction preventing the Postal Service from implementing the administration’s mail-voting order, as North Carolina began mailing ballots and other states prepare to follow.

- **Supreme Court clears path for lower GOP television-ad rates.** The Court temporarily halted a lower-court ruling, allowing Republican campaign committees to seek the same discounted broadcast-ad rates available to candidates ahead of the midterm elections.

- **Missouri asks the Supreme Court to allow its new congressional map.** The state appealed after litigation over Trump-backed districts, placing another redistricting dispute before the Court during an election year.

- **U.S. military strikes Iranian oil tankers after attacks on Navy vessels.** The military said it disabled two tankers and destroyed an empty tanker after warships were targeted by missiles, further raising the stakes for U.S. forces and commercial shipping near the Strait of Hormuz.

## International News

- **Russia announces a three-day pause in strikes on Kyiv.** Europe: Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said the pause would begin at midnight and was tied to planned visits by U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner; Ukraine reported overnight attacks on Kyiv and Boryspil before the stated pause.

- **Nepal flood rescuers recover two more workers alive.** South Asia: Teams pulled a Chinese man and a woman from separate sites 10 days after catastrophic flooding; authorities continued searching for thousands of missing people, including many linked to hydropower projects.

- **Namibia’s U.S.-financed HIV support will be phased down.** Southern Africa: The countries said Washington would provide $45 million in fiscal 2027 before transitioning to technical cooperation, following a dispute over proposed health-data and biological-specimen sharing.

- **United Nations endorses an Equal Earth map projection.** Global: The General Assembly approved a nonbinding resolution encouraging the use of a projection that more accurately represents the relative size of continents; it passed 164-1, with six abstentions.

- **Iran conflict again affects oil shipping.** Middle East: The reported U.S. strikes on Iranian tankers followed attacks on U.S. naval vessels, while limited traffic through the Strait of Hormuz continued to keep a major global energy route under pressure.
