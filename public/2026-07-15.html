Executive Summary

  • U.S.-Iran tensions escalated again overnight, with Washington reimposing a naval blockade on Iran and Tehran threatening to disrupt broader Middle East energy exports.
  • In Washington, President Donald Trump said ICE should keep using traffic stops despite a new suspension after two deadly shootings tied to immigration enforcement.
  • European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen used a Kyiv visit to renew military backing for Ukraine as Russian strikes killed civilians and Ukraine reshuffled top officials.
  • Markets entered Wednesday with support from softer U.S. inflation data and earnings, while oil stayed elevated on renewed supply-risk fears tied to the Strait of Hormuz.
  • In technology and AI, ASML lifted its 2026 outlook on stronger chip demand, while new warnings emerged about AI's effect on prices, jobs, and intellectual-property fights.

U.S. National News

  • President Donald Trump said on July 15 that ICE should continue traffic stops, publicly undercutting a new agency suspension that followed two deadly shootings connected to immigration enforcement operations in Maine and Texas.
  • Senate support is building behind a Russia sanctions bill that was one of the late Sen. Lindsey Graham's top priorities, with lawmakers moving to preserve his measure as a response to Moscow's war in Ukraine.
  • A federal judge in Miami awarded $314 million to three Americans who said they were jailed and tortured in Venezuela before being freed in a 2023 prisoner swap, calling the abuse part of a criminal enterprise tied to Nicolas Maduro's circle.
  • E. Jean Carroll received more than $5.6 million from the 2023 sexual-abuse and defamation judgment against Donald Trump after the Supreme Court allowed the verdict to stand and the escrowed money to be released.
  • Supreme Court Justices Amy Coney Barrett and Elena Kagan told Congress that threats against the court have intensified, using a rare appearance to press for more security funding and discuss ethics concerns.

International News

  • Iran said it could halt Middle East energy exports after the United States reimposed a naval blockade and widened strikes on Iranian targets, raising the risk of a broader regional conflict and more energy-market disruption.
  • European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen visited Kyiv on Ukraine's Statehood Day and pledged deeper military and defense-industry support as Russian attacks killed civilians in several regions.
  • The death toll from the Bangkok pub fire rose to 32, with more than 70 people injured, as Thai investigators examined possible safety failures and survivors criticized the venue's emergency-exit setup.
  • Yemen's fragile truce is under new strain after Saudi and Houthi forces exchanged airstrikes, missiles, and drones in the sharpest escalation in years.
  • Spain defeated France 2-0 in the men's World Cup semifinal, reaching the final after shutting down Kylian Mbappe and extending its long unbeaten run.

Technology Industry News

  • ASML published stronger second-quarter results on July 15 and raised its 2026 outlook again, saying AI-driven demand for advanced chipmaking equipment remains strong enough to justify more capacity expansion.
  • NASA astronaut Anil Menon and Roscosmos cosmonauts Pyotr Dubrov and Anna Kikina arrived at the International Space Station for an approximately eight-month mission, a sign that U.S.-Russian space cooperation is continuing despite wider geopolitical strain.
  • China temporarily blocked helium exports, adding pressure to a supply chain already squeezed by the Iran conflict and creating another potential bottleneck for semiconductor production and some medical equipment.
  • A Massachusetts man, Mahdi Mohammad Sadeghi, was found guilty of helping route U.S. technology to Iran in violation of sanctions, in a case tied to sensitive electronic components.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • A new Associated Press analysis found that the rapid AI buildout is pushing up prices for laptops, memory chips, and electricity, adding another inflation concern for consumers and policymakers.
  • More than 200 economists, AI researchers, and technology leaders signed a Stanford-led letter saying institutions must act now to address AI's economic disruption and the risk of large-scale job displacement.
  • Apple sued OpenAI in California federal court, accusing the company and former Apple employees of misusing trade secrets as OpenAI builds out its hardware plans.
  • News organizations including The New York Times and the Daily News asked a federal judge to sanction OpenAI, arguing that the company withheld or destroyed evidence in the high-stakes copyright case over AI training data.
