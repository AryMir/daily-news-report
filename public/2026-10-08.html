Executive Summary

  • U.S. stocks traded lower this morning as rising oil prices and Treasury yields renewed inflation concerns.
  • Ukrainian authorities reported at least 33 deaths in a Russian attack on Kramatorsk. The situation is developing.
  • A federal proposal would give transit projects a funding advantage in areas with higher marriage and birth rates.
  • Medicare open enrollment begins October 15; eligible Original Medicare beneficiaries are also due a one-time $90 premium rebate this month.
  • Henderson expects sunshine and a high near 95°F, with no active weather alerts identified.

U.S. National News

  • Mortgage rates reach their highest level in nearly three years. The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate rose to 7.49% in the week ending October 2, up 0.19 percentage point, increasing borrowing costs for homebuyers.
  • FBI arrests suspect in alleged Mall of America plot. An 18-year-old Minnesota man was arrested Tuesday after an undercover weapons transaction. Court filings allege he planned a mass shooting; the allegations have not been adjudicated.
  • Democrats challenge taxpayer-funded television ads. The Democratic National Committee sued Wednesday to stop administration advertising it alleges violates the federal prohibition on using appropriations for publicity or propaganda.
  • EPA plans to repeal a methane-leak policy. The agency said Wednesday it would propose scrapping a rule targeting large methane leaks from oil and gas operations. This is a planned proposal, not a completed repeal.
  • Transportation Department proposes new transit-grant criteria. A Wednesday proposal would boost major transit-project grants by 10% in areas with above-average marriage and birth rates and impose tighter project deadlines.

International News

  • Ukraine reports at least 33 killed in Kramatorsk. Ukrainian authorities said a Russian bomb struck the eastern city on Thursday, calling it an attack on civilians. Russia denies targeting civilians; the reported death toll may change.
  • Chinese militia vessel sighted off Taiwan's eastern coast. Reporting published Thursday identified an August sighting alongside two other vessels, documented in a surveillance report. It was described as the first known appearance of such a craft in that sensitive area.
  • Turkey sends support to Saudi Arabia against the Houthis. Two officials said Wednesday that Turkey was providing mainly defensive and technical assistance. The move follows the August defense agreement among Turkey, Pakistan, and Saudi Arabia.
  • Britain keeps its Jerusalem building open as a mission. Britain said Thursday the building would operate as the UK Mission, Jerusalem after Israel ordered the consulate closed. It will continue work serving Palestinians while diplomatic discussions proceed.
  • Canadian writer Anne Carson wins the literature Nobel. The Swedish Academy awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature on Thursday to the poet and essayist, whose work draws on Greek and Latin classics.

Technology Industry News

  • Samsung forecasts sharply higher quarterly earnings. On October 8, Samsung estimated third-quarter sales of 195 trillion won and operating profit of 107.4 trillion won. These are preliminary company estimates, not final results.
  • UK regulator investigates Instagram Instants. Ofcom opened an investigation October 6 into whether Meta adequately assessed illegal-content and child-safety risks before launching the disappearing-content feature. Opening the investigation does not establish a violation.
  • SmartThings update expands local home-device controls. Samsung's October 5 U.S. announcement introduced SmartThings Now and direct communication between phones and compatible hubs on the same Wi-Fi network. Samsung says this can keep supported controls responsive during internet disruptions.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • OpenAI begins a broader GPT-6 rollout in ChatGPT. The October 7 announcement introduced answers combining text, visuals, and interactive tools. Rollout began with paid plans, with Free and Go access scheduled to start October 8.
  • Anthropic releases Claude Haiku 5.5. Announced October 7, the small model targets high-volume tasks such as summarization and routing. Anthropic lists prices of $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for prompts up to 100,000 tokens; longer prompts cost more.
  • Anthropic expands access for verified cybersecurity teams. Its October 6 program introduced three tiers for defensive work, authorized penetration testing, and specialized testing of critical systems. Applicants face verification and security requirements appropriate to the access requested.

Medicare & Medical News for Seniors

  • One-time $90 Medicare premium rebates begin this month. CMS says most eligible beneficiaries will receive direct deposits around October 8, while paper checks follow later. The payment applies to certain Original Medicare Part B beneficiaries; Medicare Advantage enrollees are excluded. Eligibility questions can go to 1-800-MEDICARE.
  • Medicare open enrollment begins October 15. The enrollment window runs through December 7 for 2027 health and drug coverage. Plan comparisons are available now, giving beneficiaries time to review premiums, covered medicines, and provider networks before making changes.
  • High-deductible Medigap threshold set for 2027. CMS announced a $3,050 annual deductible effective January 1 for the high-deductible versions of Plans F, G, and J. This is an announced amount, not a proposal, and excludes premiums.
