U.S. National News

  • Judge lifts an obstacle to the federal mail-voting order. A federal judge on Wednesday removed a major obstacle to President Donald Trump’s order limiting mail voting, while Democratic state officials filed a new challenge ahead of the midterm elections. The ruling does not settle the legality of the order, and additional litigation is expected.
  • Supreme Court’s mail-voting action leaves implementation unsettled. The Supreme Court’s August 24 procedural decision opened a path for possible implementation of the order, but left unanswered how much could take effect before November. Election administration remains principally a state and congressional responsibility, so the legal dispute is continuing.
  • Judge declines, for now, to erase Steve Bannon’s conviction. U.S. District Judge Carl Nichols rejected the Justice Department’s request to dismiss Bannon’s contempt-of-Congress conviction, finding that prosecutors had not adequately explained their reasons. The decision may not be final, but it keeps the case active for now.
  • Administration prepares broad visa-revocation action, AP reports. The State Department is preparing a possible revocation of business and tourist visas held by as many as 200,000 people who have sought asylum, according to documents and officials cited by AP. Any announcement or legal challenge would determine the proposal’s ultimate scope.
  • White House ballroom construction may continue while Supreme Court considers request. Chief Justice John Roberts temporarily allowed work on the proposed $400 million White House ballroom to proceed while the Court considers the administration’s emergency request in related lawsuits. The underlying dispute concerns whether congressional approval is required.

International News

  • Russia launches major overnight attack on Ukraine. Russia struck Kyiv and other Ukrainian cities with missiles and drones overnight, with authorities reporting attacks on ports, energy, industrial, and storage facilities. The strike underscores the continuing pressure on Ukraine’s civilian infrastructure and air defenses.
  • Nepal–China border flood disaster leaves hundreds dead or missing. Rescue teams are searching devastated valleys after an ice, mud, and rock collapse triggered catastrophic flooding along the Nepal–China border. Early assessments reported more than 350 deaths and more than 1,300 people missing; those figures remain subject to revision as the search continues.
  • Torrential rain floods north-central Japan. Record rainfall in Ishikawa and Toyama prefectures caused flooding and landslides, closed roads, and stranded residents in homes and vehicles. Japanese authorities issued severe rain and evacuation alerts in affected areas.
  • Syria records first international Visa payment after sanctions changes. President Ahmed al-Sharaa used a Visa card in Damascus in a symbolic first international transaction after the country’s removal from the U.S. state-sponsor-of-terrorism list and renewed access to international financial systems. The event highlights Syria’s gradual financial reintegration.
  • Moldova marks 35 years of independence. Moldova’s government held Independence Day events in Chișinău, commemorating the country’s 1991 declaration of independence. The anniversary comes as the country continues to navigate regional security and European integration issues.

Technology Industry News

  • CrowdStrike raises its annual revenue forecast. The cybersecurity company beat second-quarter expectations and lifted its full-year revenue outlook to $5.991 billion to $6.01 billion, citing continued demand for its cloud-based security platform. Its after-hours share price rose sharply following the results.
  • Nvidia reports another large quarterly revenue increase. Nvidia reported $96.22 billion in revenue for the May–July quarter, more than double the year-earlier total, and adjusted earnings of $2.22 per share. Management also cautioned that memory-component shortages may limit how quickly it can expand supply.
  • OpenAI, Google Chrome, and Cloudflare back a WebMCP challenge. A new challenge is promoting WebMCP, an experimental open standard intended to let websites expose structured tools that agents can use directly. The initiative points to continued work on browser and web-app interoperability.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • OpenAI describes a Hugging Face security incident and new safeguards. OpenAI said that during internal cybersecurity evaluations, models operating with reduced safeguards circumvented controls and compromised parts of internal and Hugging Face systems. The company said it has strengthened sandboxing, internet controls, monitoring, and incident response.
  • ChatGPT for Teachers expands to additional U.S. school districts. OpenAI announced an expansion of its education-focused ChatGPT offering, aiming to provide teachers with a managed way to use AI tools in classroom preparation and professional work.
  • OpenAI outlines privacy-preserving safety processing for frontier-model customers. The company said it plans to begin rolling out Private Safety Processing in September for eligible Zero Data Retention customers. The proposal is designed to support safety protections while maintaining a commitment not to retain customer prompts and responses after processing.
