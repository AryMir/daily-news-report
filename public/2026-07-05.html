2026-07-05

Executive Summary

  • President Donald Trump’s America 250 speech went ahead after a weather delay in Washington, turning a national anniversary into a sharply political event.
  • Severe heat and storms disrupted holiday travel and celebrations across the eastern U.S., while a Wisconsin boat capsizing killed three children.
  • In global news, Iran’s leadership turned out publicly for funeral prayers for Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, while Russia’s latest heavy strike on Kyiv kept pressure on Ukraine.
  • Markets are on a long holiday weekend, so the latest available U.S. stock close is Thursday, July 2: the Dow hit a record, while the Nasdaq fell on a chip-stock selloff.
  • Henderson, Nevada is heading into dangerous desert heat, with today’s forecast near 106°F and an Extreme Heat Watch posted in the Las Vegas area.

U.S. National News

  • President Donald Trump marked the nation’s 250th anniversary with a speech on the National Mall after thunderstorms forced a roughly two-hour evacuation, and the address blended patriotic themes with attacks on political opponents.
  • Severe weather and a heat wave disrupted July Fourth events across the Midwest and Northeast; in Wisconsin, three children died after a boat capsized on Lake Geneva during a sudden storm, and widespread outages hit multiple states.
  • The House left Washington early after Republicans deadlocked over demands to tie President Trump’s SAVE America Act to other legislation, underscoring continued instability inside the GOP conference.
  • The Supreme Court ruled that states may continue counting mail ballots that arrive after Election Day if they are postmarked on time, rejecting a Trump-backed legal challenge before the 2026 midterms.
  • The June jobs report showed U.S. employers added just 57,000 jobs, a sign that hiring remains cautious and that the labor market is losing momentum.

International News

  • Iran’s top political and military figures attended funeral prayers in Tehran for Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, projecting regime unity, while his successor Mojtaba Khamenei remained out of public view.
  • Russia’s large missile-and-drone attack on Kyiv killed at least 21 people and wounded many more, one of the deadliest recent strikes on the Ukrainian capital.
  • Ukrainian and Russian officials both reported new attacks in and around Crimea as Presidents Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Vladimir Putin held separate calls with President Trump on prospects for ending the war.
  • Venezuela’s earthquake disaster is worsening into a broader humanitarian and medical crisis, with AP reporting more than 2,295 dead, over 11,000 injured, and hospitals under severe strain.
  • A new security report cited by AP says Russia likely used “shadow ships” to launch drones over parts of Europe in a covert campaign aimed at testing defenses and disrupting civilian and military infrastructure.

Oil stayed near prewar levels as traders focused on improving Middle East supply flows and diplomacy with Iran.

Technology Industry News

  • The tech trade turned volatile before the holiday weekend: the Nasdaq fell 0.8% on July 2, while the semiconductor ETF SOXX dropped 5.6%, with Sandisk down 14.1% and Micron off 5.5%.
  • Apple is seeking Trump administration approval to buy memory chips from blacklisted Chinese supplier ChangXin Memory Technologies, according to a Reuters report carried by Yahoo Finance, highlighting how AI-era hardware demand is colliding with U.S. export controls.
  • OpenAI’s reported proposal to give the U.S. government a 5% stake, if pursued, would mark an extraordinary new overlap between frontier tech companies and Washington industrial policy.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • OpenAI has discussed giving the U.S. government a 5% ownership stake, the Financial Times reported; Reuters said it could not immediately verify the report, but the idea reflects rising pressure to show the public a direct financial stake in AI.
  • Anthropic’s Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models are back online after the U.S. lifted export controls imposed over cybersecurity fears, ending a weeks-long disruption for one of the sector’s most closely watched model launches.
  • South China Morning Post reported on July 5 that ByteDance and Alibaba are disabling or curbing some AI agents in China, a sign that companies are pulling back where reliability, safety, or oversight concerns remain unresolved.
