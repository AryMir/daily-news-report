  • U.S. markets are closed on Saturday, so the latest available numbers are Friday, July 17 closes: the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq all fell, while crude oil jumped on Middle East supply fears.

U.S. National News

  • President Donald Trump used a primetime address on Thursday, July 16, to repeat unsupported claims about the 2020 election and press for stricter voting laws ahead of the midterms. AP reported that the speech again disputed his 2020 loss without showing evidence that vote totals were altered.
  • The Trump administration is reviving a broader "public charge" rule that can weigh use of benefits such as Medicaid, food stamps, and housing aid against some green-card applicants. Immigration advocates say the rule could discourage legally eligible families from seeking help.
  • An Associated Press analysis found that federal disaster-aid decisions under Trump have moved more slowly on average and have favored Republican-led states over Democratic-led ones. AP reported that about 80% of requests from Republican governors have been approved versus about 60% from Democratic governors.
  • The federal government replaced the slavery exhibit at George Washington's home in Philadelphia with a version historians and local officials say softens the brutality of slavery. The change follows a court fight over the National Park Service's effort to reinterpret the display.
  • Supreme Court Justices Amy Coney Barrett and Elena Kagan made a rare appearance before Congress to describe growing threats against the court and to seek more money for security. Barrett said she has worn a bulletproof vest, while Kagan warned that threats and political pressure are rising.

International News

  • The United States and Iran exchanged fresh strikes on Saturday, July 18, over control and security in the Strait of Hormuz. AP reported that Kuwait said its air defenses intercepted Iranian missiles but that some infrastructure was still damaged.
  • The European Union and Britain announced coordinated sanctions against Russian intelligence officers, hackers, and linked companies over a long-running cyberespionage campaign. Officials said the measures target actors tied to attacks on governments and critical infrastructure across Europe.
  • Ukraine and the European Union signed a new defense industrial partnership to expand joint weapons production as Russia's war grinds on. The agreement aims to speed drone, anti-drone, and eventually anti-ballistic missile production with closer European support.
  • More than 500 Rohingya refugees are feared dead after two boats reportedly capsized in the Bay of Bengal. The U.N. refugee agency and the International Organization for Migration said the tragedy reflects worsening desperation among people fleeing Myanmar and overcrowded camps in Bangladesh.
  • Israeli strikes in Gaza killed at least a dozen people over two days, including police officers and civilians, despite an ongoing ceasefire with Hamas. The latest attacks added to doubts about how stable the ceasefire remains.

Technology Industry News

  • TSMC said on Thursday, July 16, that it will invest another $100 billion to expand manufacturing capacity in the United States. The company said strong customer demand, especially from AI, is driving the move, which adds to its already large Arizona buildout.
  • New York imposed a one-year moratorium on new hyperscale data centers while regulators study energy, climate, water, and community impacts. The order has made the state a focal point in the national debate over how fast AI infrastructure should expand.
  • SpaceX aborted a Starship launch at the pad on Thursday after engine trouble triggered an automatic stop. Elon Musk said two Raptor engines will be replaced before the next launch attempt.
  • Netflix reported higher second-quarter revenue and profit, but its shares fell after management gave a softer growth outlook and said it would release its "What We Watched" engagement report less often. Investors treated the weaker forward guidance as a warning that growth is slowing.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • Chinese startup Moonshot unveiled its Kimi K3 model, and AP reported that the release caught much of the U.S. tech industry off guard. Moonshot said the model has 2.8 trillion parameters and is competitive with leading systems from Anthropic and OpenAI in some tasks.
  • Chinese President Xi Jinping called for broader global AI rules at the World Artificial Intelligence Conference and said China will provide 5,000 AI training opportunities to developing countries over five years. AP also reported that China plans to open an AI meteorology tool to 30 countries.
  • More than 200 economists, AI researchers, and technology executives signed a Stanford-led statement warning that institutions "must act now" on AI's economic effects. The signers said the technology could raise living standards but also create large-scale job displacement without better guardrails.
  • The AI infrastructure boom is starting to show up in consumer costs. AP reported that heavy spending by Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft on data centers is helping drive up prices for memory chips, electronics, and electricity.
