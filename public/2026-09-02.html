2026-09-02

Executive Summary

  • Congress has approved a short-term funding measure to avert a government shutdown, while a federal appeals court restored Bowe Bergdahl’s military conviction.
  • Former Tropical Storm Edouard is bringing dangerous inland flooding to Texas, and a new investigation documents the financial toll of fraud on U.S. households.
  • Fighting involving the United States and Iran has intensified across the Gulf, placing renewed pressure on energy markets and regional security.
  • U.S. stocks finished lower Tuesday as oil and bond yields climbed; WTI crude settled above $90 per barrel.
  • Major technology companies are managing service disruptions and leadership changes, while AI developers are reporting new model releases and tighter cyber safeguards.

U.S. National News

  • Congress sends temporary government funding measure to the president. The House approved a stopgap bill, 370-48, to keep federal agencies funded into early December after the Senate had already passed it. The measure is intended to prevent a funding lapse when the fiscal year ends in September.
  • Tropical Depression Edouard floods parts of Texas. The former tropical storm weakened after landfall near the Texas-Louisiana border but continued to bring severe rain, closed schools and flooded roads. Areas northwest of Beaumont received roughly 10 to 21 inches of rain in hours, keeping flood danger high as the system moved inland.
  • Investigation finds record scam losses and gaps in victim support. Americans reported $15.9 billion in losses to the Federal Trade Commission last year, according to an Associated Press and FRONTLINE investigation. The reporting found many victims face a difficult recovery process and can be targeted again by recovery scams.
  • Appeals court says civilian judge could not vacate Bowe Bergdahl conviction. A three-judge federal appeals panel ruled that military tribunals, rather than a federal district court, have authority over the former Army soldier’s court-martial conviction for desertion. The decision directs dismissal of his challenge in civilian court.
  • Lawmakers seek explanation for Army drone-warfare decision. A bipartisan group in Congress asked the Army to explain why a Europe-based unit was directed to stop specializing in drone warfare. The inquiry comes as uncrewed systems have become increasingly important in modern conflicts.

International News

  • Iran fires toward Gulf states after new U.S. strikes. Iran launched attacks toward U.S. partners in the Gulf after overnight American bombardment, with Iranian officials saying a strike hit a wedding gathering. The escalation renewed concern over regional security and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.
  • China’s Xi visits Egypt to deepen regional ties. President Xi Jinping began a visit to Egypt focused on economic and diplomatic cooperation. The trip highlights China’s effort to expand its role in the Middle East and North Africa.
  • Russian strikes kill civilians in Ukraine. Russian attacks killed two Ukrainian civilians as both Moscow and Kyiv signaled a willingness to increase pressure in the war. Continued attacks on populated areas underscore the conflict’s humanitarian and security consequences for Europe.
  • Canada presses for serious trade talks with Washington. Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada is prepared to resume negotiations if the United States returns to substantive discussions. His comments followed the collapse of talks in August and a new public exchange between the two governments.
  • Chevron plans to expand Venezuelan operations. A U.S. official said Chevron is expected to broaden its activity in Venezuela following a new U.S. initiative to develop the country’s oil reserves. Any expansion would be significant for Venezuela’s energy sector but faces operational and political constraints.

Technology Industry News

  • Microsoft continues recovery from Microsoft 365 outage. Microsoft said service availability was improving after a multiday incident disrupted Outlook email and affected other Microsoft 365 services, including Teams and SharePoint. The company attributed the problem to an authentication-configuration issue and remained in extended monitoring.
  • John Ternus begins tenure as Apple chief executive. Apple’s former hardware engineering leader succeeded Tim Cook as chief executive on September 1. The transition occurs ahead of Apple’s September product event and gives a longtime product executive responsibility for the company’s next phase.
  • Taiwanese companies outline additional U.S. semiconductor investment. Taiwan’s economy minister said companies plan another $20 billion in U.S. investments, citing demand connected to advanced applications. The announcement reflects continuing efforts to expand resilient semiconductor capacity outside Taiwan.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • OpenAI says Astra meets its critical cyber-capability threshold. OpenAI said its forthcoming Astra model reached the highest cybersecurity capability category in its preparedness framework. The company said it delayed elements of release while strengthening safeguards and expects to limit access to advanced cyber functions at first.
  • Anthropic introduces Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1. Anthropic announced new models aimed at coding and knowledge-work tasks, and said their research capabilities offer an early indication of potential scientific uses. The release adds to competition among providers of advanced AI systems for professional work.
  • U.S. AI policy is a focus at the G-20 innovation gathering. U.S. officials said the country would press G-20 participants against creating new international AI regulatory bodies. The discussions reflect an ongoing policy debate over how to balance innovation, competition, and safeguards.
