Executive Summary

  • The House again approved a measure to end U.S. military action in Iran without congressional authorization; its path to becoming law remains uncertain.
  • A Kosovo war crimes court sentenced former President Hashim Thaçi to 25 years, while Saudi Arabia reported an attempted drone attack near Mecca.
  • U.S. stocks opened mixed ahead of the Federal Reserve's rate decision. Oil retreated from Tuesday's surge after a reported rise in U.S. inventories.
  • Oracle issued a large security update, and leading AI companies continued to debate how to slow or oversee advanced model development.

U.S. National News

  • House votes again to limit the Iran war. The House approved a war powers resolution intended to halt further U.S. military action in Iran without congressional approval, the third such House vote this summer. Earlier resolutions did not reach the president's desk, and this measure faces an uncertain path in the Senate.
  • Supreme Court blocks new mail ballot restrictions before the midterms. The court rejected the administration's effort to impose new postal requirements on states ahead of November's elections. Existing mail voting procedures can continue while other election related disputes proceed.
  • News helicopter crash kills three in Los Angeles. A helicopter used by NBC and Telemundo crashed Tuesday while covering a bus collision, killing two people aboard and one person on the ground, according to local officials. The cause had not been established.
  • Surgeon general nominee appears before senators. Dr. Nicole Saphier, President Donald Trump's third nominee for the position, faced a Senate confirmation hearing Wednesday. The role remains unfilled as lawmakers review her qualifications and public health views.
  • Kennedy Center board votes to close most of venue. The board approved a closure for renovations Tuesday, hours after a federal judge blocked a plan to add Trump's name to the building without congressional approval. The Justice Department appealed the ruling; Congress has allocated $257 million for repairs.

International News

  • Europe: Former Kosovo president sentenced for war crimes. A court in The Hague convicted Hashim Thaçi of murder, torture, cruel treatment and arbitrary detention during Kosovo's independence war and sentenced him Wednesday to 25 years. The decision can be appealed.
  • Middle East: Saudi Arabia reports attempted attack near Mecca. Saudi authorities said air defenses intercepted a drone south of the holy city and accused Yemen's Houthi rebels; the Houthis denied responsibility. The incident adds to regional tension around Saudi oil and shipping routes.
  • Asia: India protests naval collision with Pakistan. India summoned Pakistan's top diplomat Wednesday after a collision between the countries' naval units in the northern Arabian Sea on Tuesday. India reported no major damage and requested steps to prevent another incident; Pakistan had not immediately commented.
  • Africa: Ebola response shows mixed results in Congo. The World Health Organization reported encouraging signs in one part of eastern Democratic Republic of Congo but warned that cases are rising in another. Officials are racing to contain the outbreak across affected areas.
  • Europe: EU proposes larger firefighting capacity. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called for a new firefighting fleet after a deadly summer heat wave and fires across the bloc. The proposal would strengthen the EU's shared response to future emergencies.

Technology Industry News

  • Oracle issues extensive security fixes. Its September update includes 673 new patches spanning 17 product families and addresses more than 800 vulnerabilities when related fixes are counted. Organizations using affected Oracle products may need to prioritize remotely exploitable flaws.
  • Revolut confirms customer data disclosure. The financial technology company said an unauthorized party used a legitimate government agency email domain to make fraudulent information requests. Revolut said a limited number of customers were affected and that customer funds and its systems were unaffected.
  • Apple releases annual operating system updates. iOS 27 and macOS 27 Golden Gate became available this week, alongside updates for Apple's other device platforms. The releases include performance and interface refinements; macOS 27 is the last version scheduled to support Rosetta for Intel applications.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • Rival AI companies discuss shared safety practices. OpenAI said it has been in talks for weeks with Anthropic and Google DeepMind about AI safety. The discussions follow calls from industry leaders for more time to evaluate increasingly capable systems; no binding common plan was announced.
  • Meta rejects a coordinated development slowdown. CEO Mark Zuckerberg said each company is responsible for the safety of its own AI products, distancing Meta from rivals' calls for a joint slowdown. The disagreement shows that industry support for a common pace remains incomplete.
  • Data center operators launch a flexible power coalition. Google, Nvidia, Emerald AI and other organizations announced an alliance to encourage AI data centers to adjust electricity demand when grids are strained. The group says flexible use could ease pressure on power systems, but the approach has yet to prove its effect at scale.
