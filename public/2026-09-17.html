U.S. National News

  • Federal Reserve raises its benchmark interest rate. The Fed increased its key rate by a quarter percentage point Wednesday, its first increase in three years, as it sought to curb persistent inflation. Higher rates can gradually raise borrowing costs for mortgages, car loans and credit cards.
  • House passes a broad Russia sanctions package. Lawmakers approved the bill 262–159 Wednesday, targeting Russian officials and parts of the economy over the war in Ukraine. The measure now goes to President Donald Trump.
  • House again votes to limit U.S. military action in Iran. The chamber approved a war powers resolution Wednesday that would restrict continued military action without congressional authorization. The vote adds to pressure on the administration, although enactment remains uncertain.
  • House recesses before the midterm elections. Speaker Mike Johnson sent lawmakers home Wednesday after the chamber completed its agenda. Republican Rep. Thomas Massie criticized the decision because his proposed vote on Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and a separate effort concerning Jeffrey Epstein files were left unresolved.
  • Kennedy Center closure draws a court challenge. Fencing went up Wednesday after the arts center's board voted to close most of the building for repairs. A Democratic trustee sought an emergency hearing to block the closure, arguing that the action was unlawful.

International News

  • Canada weighs a new EU relationship. In Strasbourg, Prime Minister Mark Carney welcomed a proposal for Canada to become the European Union's first associate member, while acknowledging that the status still needs to be defined. Ottawa has already joined the EU's joint defense procurement program.
  • Japan's prime minister reshuffles her Cabinet. Sanae Takaichi made changes Thursday amid falling public support but retained key ministers. Her government faces inflation concerns and pressure over defense spending and relations with the United States.
  • Saudi Arabia reports a death after a drone interception. Saudi civil defense said debris from an intercepted Houthi drone killed one person and injured two in Taif province. The incident comes amid renewed fighting involving Yemen's Houthis and Saudi-backed forces.
  • South African police investigate a ninth woman's death. Police said Thursday that a ninth body found in the Ekurhuleni area east of Johannesburg may be connected to a series of killings. Investigators have not publicly established that the cases share a perpetrator.
  • India and Pakistan protest a naval collision. The two countries summoned diplomats after their vessels collided in the northern Arabian Sea. The incident has increased tensions between the South Asian neighbors.

Technology Industry News

  • SK hynix discusses U.S. memory-chip manufacturing with Intel. The South Korean company is in talks about making memory chips in the United States for the first time, according to people familiar with the discussions. The arrangement and location have not been finalized.
  • Meteor-reporting nonprofit suffers a cyberattack. The International Meteor Organization said an attack took much of its website offline and expects several weeks of partial downtime as it moves to new infrastructure. Fireball reporting remains a priority during the recovery.
  • Huawei introduces new chip technologies. The Chinese company unveiled semiconductor technology Thursday as it seeks to compete more closely with global chip suppliers. The announcement adds to competition over advanced computing hardware.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • OpenAI details concerning model behavior. The company disclosed six reports of unexpected or concerning behavior in its AI models and outlined a more regular reporting process. The disclosures add detail to the industry's debate over testing and incident reporting.
  • Independent AI safety reviews remain unsettled. Anthropic and OpenAI have supported placing outside evaluators inside frontier labs, but the scope of access, disclosure rights and independence have yet to be defined. Those details will determine how useful the proposed oversight is.
  • King Charles calls for human control of AI. At a gathering in Scotland on Thursday, the British monarch urged leaders from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and Nvidia to keep the technology under human control. The meeting focused on both potential benefits and safety risks.
