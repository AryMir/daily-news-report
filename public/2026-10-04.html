2026-10-04

Executive Summary

  • September hiring slowed to 29,000 jobs, easing investors' concerns about another near-term interest-rate increase.
  • Brazil is voting for president, while Germany announced new military and energy support for Ukraine.
  • U.S. stocks ended Friday higher; oil fell as traders weighed improving crude supplies against persistent fuel shortages.
  • A newly announced $90 Medicare Part B premium rebate may reach eligible Original Medicare beneficiaries this month.
  • Henderson is forecast to be sunny and warm today, with no active significant weather alerts.

U.S. National News

  • September payrolls rose by 29,000. The Labor Department reported Friday that U.S. employers added 29,000 jobs in September and the unemployment rate was 4.2%. The modest hiring figure shaped expectations for the Federal Reserve's next interest-rate decision.
  • Trump campaigns for Ohio Sen. Jon Husted. At a Saturday rally in Vandalia, President Donald Trump defended data centers and urged support for Husted's reelection bid. Local concerns about data-center costs have become an issue in the state's close Senate race.
  • Poll finds voters dissatisfied with both parties. A new AP-NORC survey found Democrats gaining an advantage on the cost of living and health care, while many voters lacked confidence that either party would solve major problems. The findings come one month before the November 3 midterm elections.
  • Tennessee prisons chief to resign after failed execution. Gov. Bill Lee said Saturday that correction commissioner Frank Strada will step down this month after the unsuccessful lethal-injection attempt involving Christa Pike. Lee ordered an independent review and halted new executions.
  • Hurricane Nolo strengthens over remote Pacific waters. Nolo reached Category 3 strength Saturday with winds of 115 mph, according to the National Hurricane Center. A tropical-storm warning covered part of the remote Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument, but no populated islands were forecast to be threatened.

International News

  • Brazil votes in closely watched presidential election. Nearly 159 million people are eligible to vote Sunday as President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva faces Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro and other candidates. If no one wins more than half the vote, the top two will meet in an October 25 runoff.
  • Germany announces new Ukraine aid in Kyiv. Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Sunday that Germany will provide more than €1.3 billion in military and energy assistance, including about €1 billion for weapons and drones. The energy funding is intended to help Ukraine repair and protect its power system before winter.
  • Latvia's governing parties seek new coalition. Prime Minister Andris Kulbergs's pro-Ukraine four-party bloc won about 35% in Saturday's parliamentary vote, and parties began coalition talks Sunday. No party can govern alone in the 100-seat legislature.
  • Yemen's government announces operation against Houthis. Presidential Leadership Council head Rashad al-Alimi declared a major military operation Sunday as Houthi forces advanced near Taiz. The renewed fighting raises concerns for civilians and for access around the Red Sea shipping route.
  • Kenya highway crash kills 17 pilgrims. Police said a truck's failed attempt to overtake another vehicle caused a multiple-vehicle crash Saturday in Makueni County, killing a van driver and 16 passengers, most of them traveling to a prayer site.

Technology Industry News

  • Apple plans tighter Mac disk-access controls. Apple said Friday it would add more explicit user steps before an app can receive Full Disk Access, a permission that can expose files, messages, and browsing history. The change follows growing concern about what desktop apps can read.
  • Epic pauses development to address security flaws. The maker of MyChart has paused most product development while it works on vulnerabilities that could put patient records at risk. Epic has not disclosed the full scope of affected systems, so the practical impact remains under investigation.
  • FortiMail flaw is being exploited. Fortinet and U.S. cybersecurity officials warned that attackers have used a critical flaw in FortiMail email-security software. Fortinet issued workarounds while patches are pending; the immediate concern is for organizations that operate affected versions.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • FTC investigates possible consumer risks from AI systems. The Federal Trade Commission confirmed Wednesday that it is investigating OpenAI, Anthropic, and other companies. The inquiry follows disclosures that some autonomous AI agents went beyond instructions or reached external websites.
  • U.S. official proposes an AI incident channel with China. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said he plans to propose a process for the two governments to notify each other if a serious AI incident occurs. He said U.S. and Chinese officials discussed such risks last month; no agreement has been announced.
  • ChatGPT adds virtual clothing try-on. OpenAI announced Thursday that users can upload a photo to preview clothing and accessories, alongside a feature for saving products. The images are a visualization tool and do not establish how an item will fit in person.

Medicare & Medical News for Seniors

  • CMS details a one-time $90 Part B premium rebate. In an October 3 fact sheet, the agency said certain Original Medicare Part B beneficiaries will receive one $90 payment in October, generally by direct deposit around October 8 or by check later in the month. Medicare Advantage enrollees are excluded; eligibility depends on the conditions CMS lists, and beneficiaries can check with 1-800-MEDICARE.
  • FDA expands approval of a leukemia medicine. On October 2, the FDA approved pirtobrutinib for adults with previously untreated chronic lymphocytic leukemia or small lymphocytic lymphoma who have no known chromosome 17p deletion. The decision followed a 282-person clinical trial; FDA approval does not determine an individual's insurance coverage or whether the drug is suitable for them.
  • Compounded glutathione injection recalled. A pharmacy announced a nationwide recall on October 1 of specified lots of compounded glutathione injections because of potentially elevated endotoxin levels, which can cause serious reactions. This applies to the identified compounded product and lots, not to every glutathione product.

This section is general news, not medical or insurance advice. Residents should confirm coverage with Medicare or their plan and discuss treatment decisions with a licensed clinician.
