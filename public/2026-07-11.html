U.S. National News

  • Severe flooding in Missouri forced the helicopter evacuation of more than 200 children and staff from Camp Taum Sauk near Lesterville, while rescuers also pulled about 20 people from a campground building that collapsed in floodwaters. Gov. Mike Kehoe declared a state of emergency as parts of the Black River region faced record flooding.
  • A rapidly growing wildfire in northeastern Los Angeles County prompted evacuation orders Friday after burning roughly 2,700 acres in remote high desert terrain. The fire spread in very hot, dry weather, and nearby Piñon Hills was placed under an evacuation warning.
  • Trump said he will let the bipartisan 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act become law without his signature, using the move to protest the Senate's refusal to pass a stricter GOP voter ID bill. The housing measure is designed to reduce construction barriers and address the country's housing shortage.
  • The Trump administration subpoenaed several New York Times reporters after their coverage of security concerns tied to the new Air Force One gifted by Qatar. The newspaper called the move a serious threat to press freedom.
  • Trump removed Democratic members Thomas Hicks and Benjamin Hovland from the bipartisan Election Assistance Commission, which helps oversee voting-system standards and election grants. The ousters add to his broader effort to reshape federal election oversight ahead of the 2026 midterms.

International News

  • Trump and Iran's new supreme leader, Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei, traded fresh threats Saturday as mediators tried to hold together a crumbling interim deal. The dispute remains centered on attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and Tehran's refusal to give up its stockpile of highly enriched uranium.
  • Russian missile and drone strikes wounded at least 11 people in Kyiv and killed two people in Odesa, according to Ukrainian officials. Ukraine said it retaliated by damaging Russian oil tankers and other vessels in the Sea of Azov.
  • China evacuated more than 1.7 million people in Zhejiang province as Typhoon Bavi brought strong winds and heavy rain across eastern China, Taiwan, and Japan's southern islands. Flights, ferries, and some rail service were suspended as authorities warned of storm surge and flooding.
  • Bangladesh relocated refugees from high-risk areas after monsoon-triggered landslides killed at least 13 Rohingya refugees, including five children, in Cox's Bazar. More rain is forecast, raising the risk of additional slides in the overcrowded camps.
  • Firefighters in southern Spain battled one of the country's deadliest wildfires on record after at least 12 people were killed in Almeria. Authorities said some victims were found in burned vehicles, suggesting they died while trying to escape the flames.

Technology Industry News

  • SK Hynix rose 12.8% in its Nasdaq debut after raising about $26.5 billion, making it the biggest foreign IPO on Wall Street. Investors are still rewarding chipmakers tied to AI memory demand, and SK Hynix said the U.S. accounted for most of its 2025 revenue.
  • The European Union formally told Meta to dismantle design features on Facebook and Instagram that it says are addictive, including infinite scroll and autoplay. The case could lead to a final ruling and a fine worth up to 6% of Meta's global annual revenue.
  • Japan's space agency carried out the first test flight of an experimental reusable rocket that safely returned after launch. The test is part of Japan's effort to lower launch costs and stay competitive in commercial space.
  • Meta is appealing the landmark Los Angeles verdict that found Instagram and YouTube liable for harming young users through social media addiction. The appeal keeps a closely watched platform-safety case active on both legal and business fronts.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • Apple sued OpenAI on Friday, accusing the ChatGPT maker of stealing trade secrets as it works on its own hardware plans around ChatGPT. The case escalates a high-stakes fight over who will control the next wave of consumer AI devices.
  • Major news outlets asked a federal judge to sanction OpenAI in a copyright case, alleging discovery misconduct and destruction of evidence. The lawsuit remains one of the most important tests of how AI companies can use copyrighted material in training.
  • AI notetaking tools are spreading quickly in workplaces, but lawyers, privacy specialists, and other professionals are warning about consent, data retention, biometric voiceprints, and privilege concerns. The main risk is that sensitive meetings can quietly become stored or reusable data.
  • Administrative assistants and secretaries are facing renewed job pressure from AI tools such as ChatGPT and Claude that can automate parts of scheduling, drafting, and research work. Some workers are adopting the tools to stay competitive, but the longer-term employment outlook remains weak.
