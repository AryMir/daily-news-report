U.S. National News

Second federal judge blocks Postal Service mail-ballot restrictions. U.S. District Judge Carl J. Nichols ordered the Postal Service not to implement an Aug. 21 rule tied to President Donald Trump's executive order on mail voting. The Supreme Court is separately considering an appeal involving a similar injunction as states prepare for the November midterms.

EPA moves toward repealing power-plant greenhouse-gas limits. The Environmental Protection Agency is expected to eliminate Biden-era limits on emissions from coal- and natural-gas-fired power plants. Administrator Lee Zeldin says the change would reduce industry costs, while environmental and public-health groups have promised legal challenges.

House speaker says proposed $5,000 payments require Congress. Speaker Mike Johnson said President Trump's proposed $5,000 payment to every American adult would need congressional approval. The proposal, conditioned on Republicans retaining control of Congress, would cost more than $1 trillion if broadly implemented.

Judge rules planned 50% FEMA staffing reduction unlawful. U.S. District Judge Susan Illston found that a Department of Homeland Security plan to cut Federal Emergency Management Agency staffing in half violated congressional protections. Labor groups had challenged the proposed reduction, arguing it would weaken the nation's disaster-response capacity.

Election groups organize legal and volunteer response networks. Democratic state parties announced a plan to recruit 10,000 volunteers to monitor polling places, while civil-rights groups and legal scholars prepared court strategies and protest plans. The effort reflects rising concern about federal involvement in voting and ballot counting during the midterms.

International News

NATO pledges more Ukraine support after strikes near Poland. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte said Russian drone attacks near the Ukraine-Poland border would prompt greater support for Kyiv and stronger defenses along the alliance's eastern flank. One strike hit a train station shortly after visiting senior officials had passed through it.

Houthi advances tighten pressure on Red Sea shipping and Saudi oil exports. Yemen's Houthi rebels seized Greater and Lesser Hanish islands near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, while damage to Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline is expected to take three to five weeks to repair. The developments further restrict alternatives to the Strait of Hormuz and add pressure to global energy prices.

Search continues after migrant boat disaster south of Crete. Greek authorities rescued 51 people after a vessel traveling from Libya took on water, but one person died and 20 remained missing. Coast guard vessels, a helicopter, a drone, commercial ships, and the European border agency Frontex joined the search.

South African highway crash kills at least 21. Two minibus taxis collided head-on before an SUV struck them on the N1 highway in Western Cape Province. Four other people were seriously injured, and authorities opened a culpable-homicide investigation into the cause.

China rejects call to curb its AI capabilities. China's Foreign Ministry criticized Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's call for continued restrictions on advanced chips and chipmaking equipment sold to China. The exchange comes before a planned Sept. 24 meeting between President Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping where AI governance is expected to be discussed.

Technology Industry News

ClickFix malware attacks spread across Windows and Mac computers. Attackers are compromising legitimate websites and using fake CAPTCHA prompts to persuade visitors to paste malicious commands into Windows Run, PowerShell, or the macOS terminal. Security researchers say the low-cost technique has become a mainstream way to deliver information-stealing malware.

Android introduces secure password-manager transfers. A new Android credential-transfer system can move passwords and passkeys directly between supported apps without creating an unencrypted export file. Google Password Manager, 1Password, Bitwarden, and Dashlane currently support the feature, which works through Google Play Services on Android 8.0 and later.

Apple enters the foldable-phone market with iPhone Duo. Apple's first foldable phone starts at $1,999 and combines a 5.4-inch outer display with a 7.6-inch inner screen. The device uses the A20 Pro chip, a titanium hinge, and software designed for split-screen use, and is scheduled to go on sale next month.

United Launch Alliance faces renewed ownership questions. Financial pressure and delays around the Vulcan rocket have revived speculation that Boeing and Lockheed Martin could sell their jointly owned launch company. ULA is preparing to return Vulcan to flight as competition from SpaceX and newer launch providers intensifies.

Artificial Intelligence News

Industry leaders call for slowing frontier-model development. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei urged companies to reduce the pace of capability gains while devoting more resources to safeguards; OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and xAI leader Elon Musk expressed support for slowing down. The warnings renewed debate over whether voluntary industry restraint can work without international coordination.

Trump rejects broad new AI guardrails. President Trump said the United States should avoid constraints that could weaken its competitive position against China, while acknowledging a need for some regulation. His position differs from lawmakers and technology executives seeking stronger oversight after recent reports of autonomous-agent misconduct.

Microsoft publishes a human-control code for its AI systems. Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman outlined a “Humanist AI” approach requiring people to retain meaningful control and barring systems from creating their own goals. The practical test will be whether the company accepts slower capability gains when safety principles conflict with competition.

Anthropic details and blocks sophisticated misuse of Claude. The company reported disrupting operations involving cyberespionage, surveillance, weapons-related work, and sensitive biological research. Anthropic said some campaigns used AI to execute or orchestrate substantial portions of their activity, prompting new safeguards and information-sharing with partners.

Google DeepMind releases a map of nine billion DNA variants. AlphaGenome Atlas predicts the effects of every possible single-letter change in the human genome. The free research platform could help scientists prioritize variants for study, although its predictions still require experimental and clinical validation.
