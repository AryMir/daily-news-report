2026-07-03

Executive Summary

  • U.S. hiring slowed sharply in June, with employers adding 57,000 jobs and labor-force participation falling to a more than five-year low.
  • Extreme heat is affecting major America 250 and July 4 events across the central and eastern United States, while Henderson, Nevada will be sunny and very hot.
  • U.S. stocks ended mixed in the latest available session: the Dow closed at a record high, while the Nasdaq fell as semiconductor shares weakened.
  • International news is led by Iran’s funeral ceremonies for late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, the aftermath of Venezuela’s earthquake, and renewed Russian attacks on Kyiv.
  • Technology and AI news focused on Microsoft’s new $2.5 billion AI adoption company, Tesla’s record quarterly deliveries, and continued scrutiny of AI governance and infrastructure spending.

U.S. National News

  1. U.S. job growth slows sharply in June. Reuters reported that employers added 57,000 jobs in June, below expectations, while April and May job gains were revised lower. The unemployment rate fell to 4.2%, but mainly because many people left the labor force.
  2. Extreme heat complicates America 250 and July 4 celebrations. AP reported that dangerous heat across much of the central and eastern U.S. is affecting public events as the nation marks its 250th anniversary. Cooling stations, medical support, and event changes are being used in several cities.
  3. Supreme Court ruling expands presidential control over independent agencies. Reuters reported that the Supreme Court’s decision in Trump v. Slaughter overturned a 1935 precedent and allows the president greater authority to remove leaders of independent federal agencies.
  4. Supreme Court declines to pause fine against reporter. AP reported that the Supreme Court declined to stop an $800-per-day fine against former Fox News reporter Catherine Herridge, who has refused to reveal a confidential source in a Privacy Act lawsuit.
  5. Trump administration advances $46 billion “smart wall” border project. AP reported that the administration is moving ahead with a major U.S.-Mexico border project combining steel fencing, sensors, cameras, surveillance towers, and AI-supported monitoring.

International News

  1. Iran begins funeral ceremonies for Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. AP and Reuters reported that Iran is holding dayslong funeral ceremonies for the late supreme leader, with large crowds expected and heightened security in Tehran and other religious centers.
  2. Venezuela earthquake response faces criticism as losses rise. Reuters reported that acting President Delcy Rodriguez defended the government’s response after deadly earthquakes, while international aid and civilian-led rescue efforts continue. A separate Reuters report cited Verisk’s estimate of more than $10 billion in economic losses.
  3. Russian attack on Kyiv becomes the deadliest strike on the city this year. Reuters reported that Kyiv observed a day of mourning after a major Russian missile and drone attack killed at least 30 people and injured dozens more, with rescue workers still searching rubble.
  4. U.S.-Iran talks in Qatar focused on the Strait of Hormuz. Reuters reported that technical talks in Doha focused on maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz and frozen Iranian funds. Both sides agreed to keep talking, but broader issues remain unresolved.
  5. Vatican declares SSPX bishops excommunicated. Catholic news outlets reported that the Vatican confirmed excommunications tied to the Society of St. Pius X and warned priests and lay followers about formal adherence to schism.

Technology Industry News

  1. Tesla posts record second-quarter deliveries. Reuters reported that Tesla delivered 480,126 vehicles in the second quarter, beating Wall Street estimates and raising hopes that European demand may help the company return to annual growth.
  2. Tesla shares still fell despite strong deliveries. Reuters reported that Tesla’s stock dropped about 7% after the delivery report, as investors appeared to have already priced in the good news and remained focused on longer-term risks.
  3. UK electric vehicle share reaches nearly 30% in June. Reuters reported that UK new car registrations rose 15% year over year in June, while battery-electric vehicles accounted for nearly 30% of new registrations.
  4. Technology weakness weighed on the Nasdaq. Reuters reported that semiconductor shares fell sharply in the latest session, with the chip index down more than 5%, dragging the Nasdaq lower even as the Dow rose.

Artificial Intelligence News

  1. Microsoft launches a $2.5 billion AI adoption company. Reuters reported that Microsoft is creating Microsoft Frontier Company to help large customers choose and apply AI technologies that fit their businesses.
  2. OpenAI reportedly discussed a 5% U.S. government stake proposal. Reuters, citing the Financial Times, reported that OpenAI discussed giving the U.S. government a 5% stake and proposed that other major U.S. AI firms consider similar arrangements.
  3. U.N. scientific panel warns of both AI benefits and risks. Reuters reported that a U.N.-backed independent scientific panel said AI could bring major benefits but also large risks, with the findings set to be discussed at a July 6–7 AI governance meeting in Geneva.
  4. AI spending remains a central market concern. Reuters market coverage noted that technology and chip shares were under pressure, with investors watching whether very large AI-related infrastructure spending can deliver expected returns.
