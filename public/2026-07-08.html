Optimized Daily News

2026-07-08

Executive Summary

  • U.S. and global markets turned risk-averse after President Donald Trump said the Iran ceasefire was effectively over, helping push oil sharply higher.
  • Russian strikes hit Kyiv for a second straight day, while the U.S. launched new strikes on Iran and revoked its oil-sales permit after attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • In U.S. news, a federal immigration agent fatally shot a man in Houston, and Midtown Manhattan evacuations eased after engineers said a buckled high-rise was stable enough for limited reentry.
  • Microsoft cut 4,800 jobs, including heavy reductions at Xbox, while Illinois signed a major AI-regulation bill and a new bipartisan nonprofit launched to help workers adapt to AI disruption.
  • Henderson, Nevada is headed for another dangerous heat day, with a forecast high near 111 F and gusty afternoon winds.

U.S. National News

  • A federal immigration agent fatally shot a man during an enforcement operation in Houston on July 7. AP reported the shooting happened on Canal Street and is now under investigation.
  • New York officials said a Midtown Manhattan high-rise with buckled columns and sagging floors was stable enough for some evacuations to be lifted, though the area remains under close monitoring.
  • Former Senate Republican leader Mitch McConnell remains hospitalized more than three weeks after admission for undisclosed health issues, but aides said he held substantive calls this week with GOP leaders John Thune and John Barrasso.
  • A lawsuit filed Tuesday alleges U.S. immigration agencies shared confidential asylum information about Iranian detainees with Iran, a claim the Department of Homeland Security denied.
  • A new AP-NORC poll found sharp divides among American Jews over Israel and Gaza, with religiously affiliated Jews generally more attached to Israel and religiously unaffiliated Jews more critical of Israel’s military actions.

International News

  • At the NATO summit in Ankara, Trump again pressed for U.S. control of Greenland and said dealing with Iran was a waste of time after fresh maritime attacks in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Russian attacks killed at least four people across Ukraine and struck Kyiv for a second straight day, extending pressure on the capital and other regions.
  • The U.S. military struck Iran early Wednesday and revoked Tehran’s ability to openly sell crude oil after three ships were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Large funeral processions continued for Iran’s late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, with heavy crowds reported in Tehran and Najaf.
  • Hamas said it was dissolving its government in Gaza and transferring authority to a U.N.-backed committee as part of the current ceasefire framework.

Stocks weakened as investors reassessed geopolitical risk after new U.S.-Iran fighting and Trump’s comments on the ceasefire. Oil moved sharply higher, while technology and AI shares remained a major drag on sentiment, especially in the Nasdaq.

Technology Industry News

  • Microsoft said it is cutting 4,800 jobs, about 2.1% of its global workforce, including 1,600 Xbox employees as it restructures the gaming business.
  • A Google executive told an Australian inquiry that YouTube would keep online a video falsely claiming a survivor of Sydney’s December antisemitic massacre was a crisis actor, saying the video met platform standards.
  • Prediction-market company Polymarket is mounting a well-funded push back into the U.S. market, trying to persuade regulators and customers that its new onshore operation is more disciplined than its offshore past.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker signed the Artificial Intelligence Safety Measures Act, adding transparency and accountability requirements for the biggest AI models and strengthening the state-led push for AI oversight.
  • AI-related stocks helped drag markets lower worldwide on Tuesday, with Samsung down 6.9% in Seoul and the Nasdaq sliding 1.2% as investors questioned how long the AI rally can outrun expectations.
  • A new bipartisan nonprofit, RAISE US, launched with more than $500 million to fund education and training programs aimed at helping workers adapt to AI-driven job disruption.
