2026-08-24

Executive Summary

  • The Supreme Court cleared the way for President Donald Trump to move ahead with his executive order restricting mail-in voting, while a separate immigration push could revoke up to 200,000 visitor visas tied to asylum claims.
  • A fast-moving wildfire near Reno has destroyed at least 32 homes and still has tens of thousands of residents under evacuation orders, even after firefighters reported better containment.
  • Canada is preparing retaliatory tariffs after trade talks with Washington collapsed, while Iran warned of consequences after the United States announced a broader sanctions push.
  • U.S. stocks finished mixed on Monday, August 24, as investors braced for Nvidia earnings and Jackson Hole signals; oil fell sharply after new U.S. sanctions on Iran.
  • Henderson, Nevada was 91 F at 3:20 PM PDT, with a high near 107 F, a 20% storm chance, and an Extreme Heat Warning in effect through Friday evening.

U.S. National News

  • The Supreme Court on Monday allowed the Trump administration to proceed with its executive order restricting mail-in voting while legal fights continue over how much of the policy can take effect before the midterm elections. Oregon officials said they will keep fighting to protect their vote-by-mail system.
  • The Trump administration is preparing what AP described as the largest mass visa revocation in U.S. history, targeting up to 200,000 business and tourism visas held by people who later sought asylum. The move would not itself deport people, but it would strip their visitor status and is likely to face legal challenges.
  • Firefighters reported 27% containment on the Hawk Fire near Reno after it destroyed at least 32 homes and damaged six more. Evacuation orders were reduced but still covered roughly 63,000 people, and officials warned that dry weather and gusty winds could reignite growth.
  • A federal judge in New York vacated a Trump administration policy that had suspended immigration visa processing for nationals of 75 countries. The ruling said the State Department had overstepped its authority by blocking cases based mainly on nationality.
  • Sean Grayson, the former Illinois deputy convicted in the 2024 killing of Sonya Massey, died in prison while serving a 20-year sentence. Massey’s killing became a national flashpoint over police use of force and prompted reforms in Sangamon County, Illinois.

International News

  • Canada said it will announce retaliatory tariffs on Tuesday after trade negotiations with the United States collapsed. Prime Minister Mark Carney signaled a more targeted response as President Trump threatened new 50% tariffs on Canadian autos, steel and auto parts.
  • Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent unveiled a broader sanctions push against Iran and warned other countries to cut financial ties with Tehran or risk U.S. retaliation. Iran responded that any support for the sanctions would bring consequences, as the standoff over the Strait of Hormuz continues.
  • Israeli strikes in Gaza killed at least four people on Monday, including two children, according to AP, despite a recent U.S. request that Israel scale back military operations. The latest attacks hit multiple areas including Bureij, Shati, Deir al-Balah and Khan Younis.
  • Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy publicly detailed his split with dismissed Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov and said Ukraine remains short of Patriot missiles as Russian attacks continue. He also warned that internal political friction and supply limits are complicating the war effort.
  • A gang coalition attacked the farming community of Kenscoff above Port-au-Prince, Haiti, killing multiple residents and burning homes. Local officials said the toll was still unclear because access and communications were badly disrupted.

Wall Street opened a busy week with a mixed finish as investors positioned for Nvidia earnings, inflation data and Jackson Hole remarks from Fed Chair Kevin Warsh. Oil moved the other way, dropping after the new Iran sanctions announcement and helping ease bond-yield pressure.

Technology Industry News

  • Taiwanese prosecutors charged nine people, including one Nvidia manager and two former Super Micro employees, with illegally exporting high-end AI servers to mainland China. Authorities said 74 restricted servers reached China and 56 more were intercepted in Taiwan.
  • Alibaba said quarterly profit fell 75% from a year earlier as capital spending on AI infrastructure surged. Revenue still rose 9%, and AI cloud and compute revenue increased 45%, showing how costly the current buildout remains.
  • TikTok reached a $400 million settlement with the U.S. Justice Department over allegations that it violated children’s privacy laws. The deal closes a 2024 lawsuit tied to data collection from users under age 13.
  • Dutch regulators fined Uber 825 million euros, or about $964 million, saying the company used automated software to suspend driver accounts without enough human review. Uber said it will appeal the ruling.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • Nvidia is spending about $6 billion on a sweeping deal with startup Poolside to strengthen a U.S.-based open-weight AI ecosystem. The agreement includes technology licensing, talent moves and a roughly $1 billion equity investment in Poolside.
  • OpenAI said one of its upcoming models, Astra, may have critical cybersecurity capabilities under its Preparedness Framework. The company said it added extra monitoring for tool-enabled use after internal evaluations on August 7.
  • OpenAI introduced ChatGPT for Teens, a version aimed at users ages 13 to 17 with tighter restrictions around self-harm, sexual content and other sensitive topics. The company said the product is designed to support learning without simply doing students’ work for them.
  • OpenAI said ChatGPT Ads will expand next week to 31 European countries, widening the company’s advertising rollout beyond earlier test markets. Advertisers will initially buy access through OpenAI’s sales and partner channels before broader self-service access arrives.
  • OpenAI’s developer documentation says the Assistants API beta will be removed on August 26, 2026, one year after the deprecation notice. Developers are being directed to the Responses API as the replacement path.
