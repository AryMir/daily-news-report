Executive Summary

  • U.S.-Iran tensions escalated again, with the U.S. military saying it struck about 140 targets in Iran and oil prices moving higher as markets focused on the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Washington opened the week with political disruption after Sen. Lindsey Graham’s death, Sen. Mitch McConnell’s disclosure that a fall caused his hospitalization, and a Justice Department move to subpoena New York Times reporters.
  • Internationally, a Bangkok music-bar fire killed at least 27 people, 14 nations and the EU reaffirmed the 2016 South China Sea ruling against China’s claims, and Typhoon Bavi continued to batter eastern China.
  • Markets were mixed: the major U.S. indexes ended modestly higher at the latest available close on Friday, July 11, while WTI crude jumped Monday morning on Middle East supply fears.
  • Henderson starts hot again, with a current reading near 88°F, a forecast high near 102°F, and a 30% chance of isolated to scattered thunderstorms this afternoon.

U.S. National News

  • The Senate returned to Washington under an uncertain agenda after Sen. Lindsey Graham’s death. South Carolina Gov. Henry McMaster must appoint a temporary replacement, and the state is preparing a special Republican primary on Aug. 11.
  • Sen. Mitch McConnell said a fall led to his hospitalization, ending weeks of speculation about his health. The Kentucky Republican made the disclosure Sunday as questions continue about his role in a narrowly divided Senate.
  • The Justice Department subpoenaed New York Times journalists over reporting on the Qatari-gifted Air Force One, prompting renewed criticism from press-freedom advocates. The AP described it as a significant escalation in the Trump administration’s conflict with the media.
  • Residents in Philadelphia-area neighborhoods spent Sunday cleaning up after a string of microburst storms. AP reported downed trees and power lines, flooded streets, and at least one building collapse that crushed cars and blocked trolley tracks.
  • A dangerous heat wave is still gripping large parts of the country. AP said temperatures 15 to 25 degrees above normal could affect up to two-thirds of the continental U.S., with especially risky overnight heat in parts of the South and Southwest.

International News

  • The U.S. military said it struck about 140 targets in Iran after Tehran’s attacks in Gulf states and near the Strait of Hormuz. The new round of fighting deepened fears about shipping disruptions through one of the world’s most important energy chokepoints.
  • A fire at a Bangkok music bar killed at least 27 people and critically injured 25 others. Thai police said many victims were found trapped in windowless bathrooms near a rear exit.
  • Fourteen countries and the European Union reaffirmed the 2016 international ruling that found no legal basis for China’s sweeping South China Sea claims. Their joint statements also rejected destabilizing actions in the disputed waters.
  • Typhoon Bavi weakened after reaching China’s eastern coast, but it still brought heavy rain, strong winds and large waves to Zhejiang and surrounding areas. Flooded roads and storm-surge damage were reported as the system moved inland.
  • Fifteen Indian tourists killed in a Vietnam speedboat accident were flown home Monday, and authorities continue investigating the captain. The accident has renewed scrutiny of tourist-boat safety near Phu Quoc.

Markets started the week with a split focus: investors were watching Middle East tensions for their effect on oil and shipping, while still treating AI spending, chip stocks, inflation data and bank earnings as the main drivers for equities. The latest available Friday close for the major indexes was still positive, but Monday coverage from MarketWatch and The Wall Street Journal showed fresh pressure on tech-heavy trade tied to AI.

Technology Industry News

  • AOL owner Bending Spoons was one of today’s headline technology stories after moving ahead with a $1.7 billion IPO. The deal is a notable test of investor demand for established internet and software assets.
  • SK Hynix rose 12.8% in its Wall Street debut Friday after a $26.5 billion ADR sale, the largest-ever U.S. IPO by a foreign company according to AP. The move underscored how strongly memory-chip demand is still being linked to AI infrastructure buildouts.
  • Meta appealed the Los Angeles verdict that found Instagram and Facebook were designed to hook young users without sufficient regard for their well-being. The case remains one of the most closely watched legal fights over social-media product design.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • AP reported that the global AI buildout is becoming a fresh inflation problem. Heavy spending on data centers, memory chips, processors and electricity is pushing up prices for some laptops, devices and power use.
  • Utilities, regulators and clean-energy advocates are increasingly clashing over how to power AI data centers. AP highlighted efforts to let large power users back dedicated renewable projects instead of relying only on new gas generation.
  • AI stocks opened the week under pressure. The Wall Street Journal reported Nasdaq-100 futures down about 1% before the bell, SK Hynix down 15% in Seoul after its blockbuster debut, and Micron off about 5% premarket as investors questioned how much room remains in the AI trade.
