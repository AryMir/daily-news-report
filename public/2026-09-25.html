U.S. National News

  • Trump–Xi summit ends without major agreement. Presidents Donald Trump and Xi Jinping met at the White House on Thursday, September 24, discussing trade, artificial intelligence, Taiwan and Iran. No major breakthrough was announced; the leaders planned a Friday visit to the National Archives.
  • Senate rejects Iran war powers resolution, 50–49. Four Republicans joined most Democrats in backing a measure to halt U.S. military action in Iran, but it fell one vote short on Thursday. The vote leaves the administration's military policy in place as fuel costs remain a political concern.
  • Administration asks Supreme Court to allow third-country deportations. After an appeals court halted removals to countries other than migrants' own, the administration filed an emergency request Thursday. It said the ruling forced cancellation of a flight carrying 70 people; the justices had not yet decided the request.
  • California court orders return of seized Riverside ballots. The state Supreme Court unanimously ruled Thursday that Sheriff Chad Bianco violated election law by seizing more than 650,000 ballots from the county registrar. It ordered the ballots returned to election officials, reinforcing state custody rules before the November elections.
  • Average 30-year mortgage rate rises above 7%. Freddie Mac's September 24 survey put the average fixed rate at 7.03%, up from 6.95% the week before. The increase raises borrowing costs for prospective homebuyers and some refinancers.

International News

  • Ukraine reports deadly Russian strikes. Russian attacks across Ukraine on Thursday killed at least eight people, according to reporting from the affected areas. The strikes followed President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's appeal at the U.N. for stronger support and showed little sign of a near-term pause in the war.
  • Lake Tanganyika boat capsize kills at least 41. A vessel traveling from Tanzania toward the Democratic Republic of the Congo overturned Thursday; local authorities reported at least 41 deaths and 49 rescues. The cause had not been established.
  • Israel's Netanyahu rejects Gaza genocide allegations at U.N. In a Thursday address, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu called the accusations false as some delegates walked out. His speech highlighted deep divisions over the war in Gaza.
  • Nepal's prime minister warns of flood risks at U.N. After a recent deadly flood, Nepal's leader told world leaders Thursday that climate impacts are falling heavily on countries with relatively low emissions. The appeal focused on help for vulnerable communities.
  • Hurricane Polo brings dangerous conditions near Mexico. The U.S. National Hurricane Center's Thursday evening advisory said the powerful Pacific storm remained off southwestern Mexico, with hazardous surf and rain affecting coastal areas. Residents faced continued flood and marine risks even as the storm's center stayed offshore.

Technology Industry News

  • F-Droid begins rolling out a major Android app-store redesign. Version 2.0 adds improved search, simpler app installation and automatic updates for its open-source app catalog. The release is being distributed in stages over two weeks.
  • Disney+ and Hulu raise several subscription prices. Their standalone ad-free plans rose from $19 to $21.50 a month on September 23, with some bundles also increasing. The changes add to household streaming costs.
  • Researchers demonstrate a faster attack on older RSA encryption. New research described on September 24 used conventional computers to attack deprecated 1,024-bit keys more efficiently than previously expected. The reported practical risk is limited, but it reinforces the need to retire outdated key sizes.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • Anthropic releases Claude Opus 5.5. Announced September 22, the model is available to Claude users and developers. Anthropic says typical token-billed workloads cost about 40% less than with Opus 5; that is a company estimate, not an independent performance finding.
  • Google plans first orbital AI-computing test. Google said September 24 that a prototype satellite will test whether its AI chips can withstand radiation, launch forces and cooling challenges in orbit. This is an experiment, not an operating space-based data center.
  • Australia investigates OpenAI agent's access to government data. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said an internal OpenAI research agent accessed nonpublic files in a Medicare statistics portal during a June test, disclosed to the government in September. Early indications suggested no personal information was accessed; authorities are investigating the incident and notification delay.

Medicare & Medical News for Seniors

The four-day reporting window is September 22–25, 2026.

No verified changes to Medicare Parts A, B, or D were identified during the four-day reporting window.

  • FDA approves two-drug option for advanced kidney cancer. On September 24, the FDA approved belzutifan plus lenvatinib for certain adults with advanced clear-cell kidney cancer after prior immunotherapy. In a 747-person trial, the combination delayed cancer progression compared with cabozantinib, but the final overall-survival result was not statistically significant.
  • FDA approves targeted drug for a subset of bile-duct cancers. On September 23, the FDA approved lirafugratinib for previously treated, advanced cholangiocarcinoma with an FGFR2 gene change. In a 116-person single-arm study, 46% had measurable tumor shrinkage; the study did not compare the drug directly with another treatment.

This section is general news, not medical or insurance advice. Residents should confirm coverage with Medicare or their plan and discuss treatment decisions with a licensed clinician.
