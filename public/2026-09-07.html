U.S. National News

  • NTSB begins Miami cargo-jet crash investigation. Federal investigators are examining why an Amazon cargo plane ran off a Miami International Airport runway and struck vehicles, killing at least five people and injuring five others. The inquiry will focus on the landing, aircraft maintenance history, and other operational evidence.
  • Ohio governor candidate targeted at county fair. Authorities said a man charged toward Democratic gubernatorial candidate Amy Acton while she was speaking at the Canfield Fair. He was jailed on disorderly-conduct and assault charges; the incident comes during a closely watched Ohio race against Republican Vivek Ramaswamy.
  • Administration renews Supreme Court bid on mail-ballot rules. The Trump administration again asked the Supreme Court to allow restrictions on mail ballots after a federal judge extended a block on the Postal Service enforcing the related executive order. The timing is consequential because states have begun mailing ballots for November's midterm elections.
  • Missouri seeks to restore new congressional map. Missouri appealed to the U.S. Supreme Court to permit its new congressional districts for the midterms. The state court had blocked the map and ordered a statewide November vote on whether it should take effect, leaving election administrators facing a compressed timetable.
  • Haitian New Yorkers mark West Indian Day amid status uncertainty. New York's Haitian community gathered for the annual West Indian Day Parade as the end of temporary protected status could affect roughly 350,000 Haitians. The observance has taken on added importance for families confronting immigration uncertainty.

International News

  • Germany: AfD victory tests established coalition barrier. Alternative for Germany won a landslide in Saxony-Anhalt's state election, falling just short of an outright majority. The outcome puts pressure on other parties' longstanding refusal to govern with the far-right party and has implications for upcoming regional votes.
  • Indonesia: Anak Krakatau ash keeps airports closed. Indonesian authorities extended closures at several airports and moved schools online in affected areas as the eruption eased. The disruption had halted nearly 3,000 flights and underscores the continuing aviation and public-safety effects of volcanic ash.
  • Ukraine: U.S. envoys hold talks in Kyiv. Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived in Kyiv for discussions aimed at restarting Russia-Ukraine negotiations after meeting President Vladimir Putin in Moscow. No breakthrough was announced, but the visits placed renewed attention on diplomatic channels.
  • Middle East: Lebanese village hit by Israeli airstrikes. Israeli strikes on a Lebanese village killed 12 people, according to the latest regional reporting. The attack adds to cross-border tensions while international officials continue to press for implementation of the Gaza ceasefire plan.
  • Caribbean/United Kingdom: Jamaica presses reparations case. Jamaica is taking a petition concerning Britain's role in slavery to King Charles III through the Privy Council process. The action is part of the country's political and legal campaign for answers and reparative measures.

Technology Industry News

  • Pentagon limits advertising tracking on military devices. The Defense Department disabled advertising tracking on government-issued phones and computers used by the armed forces, responding to concerns that commercially traded location data could be used to target personnel. The change covers major service branches and Special Operations Command.
  • Suspected breach exposes risks in identity-document verification. Reporting on a suspected breach of an identity-verification provider said a criminal site claimed access to more than 150 million U.S. and Canadian driver's licenses and passports. The FBI said it was looking into the incident, while the company identified in the reporting said it was investigating.
  • X investigates password-reset wave after X Money launch. X said attackers appeared to be mass-triggering password-reset emails for user accounts after the rollout of its payments service. The company said it had not found evidence of successful breaches or mass account takeovers, but users were urged to use two-factor authentication.
  • Federal regulator reviews Tesla Cybercab deployment. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened an investigation into Tesla's launch of Cybercabs on public roads in Austin without steering wheels or pedals. The agency said it will examine the certification process and technical data relied upon by Tesla.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • Anthropic introduces Claude Fable 5.1. Anthropic announced Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1, describing them as new models for coding and knowledge work. The company said Fable 5.1 is available to Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise users as well as through its developer platform and cloud marketplaces.
  • Anthropic outlines enterprise safety-and-privacy service. The company announced Enterprise Frontier Safeguards, a planned system intended to combine customer-controlled data storage with monitoring for model misuse. It said the service will roll out in phases later this fall and was developed with more than 100 customers.
  • Google releases Gemini 3.8 Flash and restricted cyber model. Google announced Gemini 3.8 Flash and Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber. The cybersecurity variant is being offered to trusted defenders through the Fairwind Program for vulnerability discovery and automated patching rather than general release.
