U.S. National News

Federal court rejects order keeping Michigan coal plant open. A federal appeals court ruled Friday that the Energy Department exceeded its authority when it required the 64-year-old J.H. Campbell coal plant to remain open beyond its planned retirement. The ruling could affect similar emergency orders covering other aging power plants and the costs borne by utility customers.

House prepares to consider a broad Russia sanctions package. The House is expected to take up legislation next week that would impose additional penalties related to Russia, after the Senate approved the measure by a wide margin. Its prospects remain uncertain because some lawmakers object to provisions that would expand presidential tariff authority.

Federal assault cases against protesters often failed to produce felony convictions. An Associated Press review found that the Justice Department did not secure felony convictions against most of 102 protesters arrested on assault charges in four major cities during federal deployments last year. The results highlight the gap between the original charging decisions and the evidence accepted by juries and judges.

Judge keeps Arizona sheriff’s office under court supervision. A federal judge rejected a request to end oversight of the Maricopa County Sheriff’s Office, which began after a 2013 ruling found that Latino motorists were racially profiled during immigration enforcement under former Sheriff Joe Arpaio. The decision means compliance monitoring and court-ordered reforms will continue.

Nation marks 25 years since the September 11 attacks. Memorial ceremonies Friday included the reading of victims’ names, moments of silence, volunteer events, and a Pentagon observance attended by President Donald Trump. Federal officials also released dozens of declassified presidential intelligence briefs concerning al-Qaida and Osama bin Laden from the period before the attacks.

International News

Houthi advance raises new danger for Red Sea shipping. Iran-backed Houthi forces captured Yemen’s port of Mokha and the strategic island of Mayun, also known as Perim, near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. The advance places the group closer to a passage normally used for about 12% of global trade and compounds pressure on Saudi oil routes.

North Korea launches several short-range ballistic missiles. South Korea said missiles fired Saturday from the Wonsan area traveled about 155 miles toward the sea, one day after a trilateral U.S.-South Korea-Japan military exercise ended. Seoul said it strengthened surveillance and was sharing information with Washington and Tokyo.

Russian strikes kill five civilians and wound dozens in Ukraine. Ukrainian officials reported deaths in Zaporizhzhia and Kryvyi Rih and 35 injuries in Odesa and the surrounding region after overnight attacks. The strikes came as Russia intensified an air campaign that has also targeted Ukraine’s power grid ahead of winter.

BRICS leaders call for changes to global governance at New Delhi summit. Leaders of the emerging-economy group expressed concern about escalating Middle East tensions, criticized sanctions imposed without United Nations authorization, and renewed calls for broader Asian, African, and Latin American representation on the U.N. Security Council.

Nigeria raises concerns after two nationals are killed in South Africa. Nigeria’s Foreign Ministry said two Nigerian citizens died in separate incidents in Cape Town and Johannesburg amid heightened anti-migrant tensions. The ministry called for accountability and stronger protection of foreign residents; South African police had not publicly responded to the allegations at the time of reporting.

Technology Industry News

Apple introduces its first foldable iPhone. Apple unveiled the iPhone Duo, a $1,999 foldable model with a 7.6-inch inner screen, alongside the iPhone 18 Pro and other device updates. The launch was the first major product event led by John Ternus since he became chief executive on September 1.

Microsoft issues a record-sized security update. Microsoft’s September patch release fixed roughly 972 vulnerabilities, including 112 rated critical by one widely cited count. The unusually large release underscores both the scale of software-security work and the growing use of automated tools to find flaws.

TSMC posts record monthly revenue. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company reported August revenue of NT$514.81 billion, up 10.1% from July and 53.3% from a year earlier. The results reflect strong demand for advanced chips and data-center computing capacity.

VinFast appoints founder’s eldest son as global chief executive. The Vietnamese electric-vehicle maker named Chairman Pham Nhat Quan Anh to the additional CEO role as the loss-making company pursues restructuring and overseas growth. Leadership continuity will be important as VinFast tries to improve efficiency while expanding outside Vietnam.

Artificial Intelligence News

Anthropic details attempted misuse involving weapons, surveillance, and cyber operations. The company said it disrupted accounts that tried to use Claude across seven categories of harm, including work connected to guided weapons, biological research, espionage, scams, and influence campaigns. Anthropic said users in Houthi-controlled Yemen tested a guided rocket but did not field an operational device.

OpenAI calls for binding U.S. safety rules for powerful AI. OpenAI said voluntary industry commitments are no longer sufficient and urged Congress to establish mandatory, capability-based requirements for frontier systems. The company also endorsed four California bills addressing independent evaluations, auditor standards, protections for young people, and biological risks.

DeepSeek releases a smaller, more efficient multimodal model. DeepSeek introduced V4.1-Flash, a mixture-of-experts model designed to reduce memory and storage needs while supporting text and visual inputs. The company made it available through its API and said it will temporarily route requests for its Pro model to the new version beginning September 14.

OpenAI brings full-duplex voice conversations to developers. GPT-Live-1 is now available through the OpenAI API, allowing voice applications to listen and speak at the same time while coordinating with other models and tools. The release is intended to reduce latency and simplify the architecture of customer-service and workflow applications.
