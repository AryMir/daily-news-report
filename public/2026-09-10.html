Executive Summary

  • A proposed census change would exclude many immigrants from the population count used for representation and funding.
  • Attacks on Gulf shipping are intensifying risks to energy supplies.
  • Apple introduced its first foldable iPhone, with October availability.
  • Henderson faces dangerous afternoon heat, with a warning lasting until 8 PM.

U.S. National News

  • Administration proposes restricting the census count. The Trump administration proposed September 9 counting only U.S. citizens and permanent residents. The proposal could substantially affect states’ congressional representation and federal funding; it is not an implemented change.
  • Supreme Court again rejects Missouri’s push to use a new congressional map. The court’s September 10 action is another setback for efforts to use the Trump-backed map in November. The dispute concerns district boundaries that could affect control of the U.S. House.
  • Broccoli sprouts recalled during a multistate Salmonella investigation. Recalled Evergreen Fresh Sprouts products have expiration dates of September 7, 9, 11, 14 and 16 and were distributed in Idaho, Montana and Washington, possibly farther. Consumers should not eat the recalled sprouts; older adults face a greater risk of severe illness.
  • Appeals court hears challenge to mailed abortion medication. A federal appeals panel on September 9 questioned Louisiana’s effort to block the 2023 rule permitting mifepristone prescriptions through telemedicine and dispensing by mail. The hearing did not itself change nationwide access.
  • Los Angeles port records its busiest three-month stretch. The port reported handling 955,907 twenty-foot-equivalent container units in August, completing a record June–August period. The volume provides a snapshot of the movement of goods through a major U.S. trade gateway.

International News

  • Middle East: U.S. and Iranian attacks hit oil shipping. Iran said September 9 it attacked 10 ships near the Strait of Hormuz after U.S. forces sank five Iranian oil tankers. At least one seafarer was reported killed, and the escalation threatens already disrupted energy shipments.
  • Europe: Russian drones strike the Ukraine–Moldova border. Ukrainian and Moldovan officials said a September 9 attack on a crossing in Ukraine’s Odesa region killed two people. Damage along this frontier threatens trade and logistics routes.
  • Southeast Asia: Deadly ferry fire prompts search off the Philippines. At least five people died after a ferry caught fire off Palawan on September 9, according to the Philippine Coast Guard. Others remained missing as rescuers searched surrounding waters.
  • South Asia: WHO regional director resigns amid investigations. Saima Wazed resigned September 9 as the World Health Organization’s Southeast Asia regional head, effective immediately. Her departure follows fraud investigations by the organization and Bangladeshi authorities; allegations should not be treated as a finding of guilt.
  • Middle East/South Asia: Pakistan conveys Saudi warning to Iran. Saudi, Pakistani and Iranian sources said Pakistan urged Iran to restrain Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia. The diplomatic effort follows strikes on Saudi facilities and retaliatory attacks in Yemen, raising concern about a widening conflict.

Technology Industry News

  • Apple introduces the iPhone Duo. Announced September 9, Apple’s first foldable iPhone has a 7.6-inch inner display and starts at $1,999 in the United States. Preorders begin October 16, with availability October 23.
  • Microsoft releases September security updates. The monthly release addresses vulnerabilities across products including Windows and Office. Supported devices and business systems should receive the applicable updates through their normal update channels.
  • Google announces €13 billion Finnish infrastructure investment. The September 9 plan covers digital infrastructure, clean energy and local partnerships over the next two years. It includes support for nuclear plant life extension, wind capacity and battery storage alongside expanded computing facilities.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • Senate inquiry targets OpenAI’s Hugging Face incident. Senator Josh Hawley is launching an investigation following findings in OpenAI’s internal inquiry into the breach. The probe adds congressional scrutiny to the behavior and oversight of increasingly capable AI agents.
  • Paul Christiano joins OpenAI’s nonprofit board. OpenAI announced September 9 that the AI safety researcher will join the Foundation Board and its Safety and Security Committee. He will also serve as a non-voting observer on the commercial company’s board.
  • Anthropic researcher resigns over development safeguards. Jacob Coxon announced his departure, arguing that leading AI companies are prioritizing competition over safety. His warning reflects a researcher’s assessment of future risks, rather than evidence that the predicted harms have occurred.
