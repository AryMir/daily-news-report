U.S. National News

  • Judge orders White House press access restored. A federal judge early Thursday temporarily blocked President Donald Trump's ban on CNN, MS NOW and Politico from White House grounds. The ruling restores their access while the outlets' First Amendment lawsuit continues.
  • Trump welcomes Xi for Washington state visit. President Trump greeted Chinese President Xi Jinping at Joint Base Andrews on Wednesday evening ahead of Thursday's White House talks. Trade, technology competition and the Iran war are among the issues facing the two governments.
  • Iowa court dismisses lawsuit over 2024 poll. An Iowa judge on Wednesday rejected Trump's lawsuit against The Des Moines Register and former pollster J. Ann Selzer over a pre-election poll, finding that the claims did not meet the legal requirements for fraud or consumer deception.
  • Weinstein receives 15-year New York sentence. A Manhattan court sentenced former film producer Harvey Weinstein on Wednesday for a felony sex crime involving a 2006 assault. The sentence follows a retrial after his earlier New York conviction was overturned.
  • New unemployment claims remain low. The Labor Department reported Thursday that seasonally adjusted initial claims fell by 1,000 to 197,000 for the week ended September 19. The figure suggests layoffs remained limited nationally.

International News

  • Ukraine: Russian strikes kill at least eight. Overnight Russian missile and drone attacks killed at least eight civilians, Ukrainian officials said Thursday. The attacks came as world leaders discussed the war at the U.N. General Assembly.
  • Ethiopia: Tigray forces seize Mekelle airport. Tigrayan forces took control of the regional capital's airport Wednesday and clashed with federal forces near Afar, according to local reporting. Ethiopian Airlines suspended flights to three Tigray airports, raising concern about the 2022 peace accord.
  • Iran: President signals conditional willingness to negotiate. President Masoud Pezeshkian told the U.N. General Assembly on Wednesday that Iran was ready for dialogue but would reject pressure or threats. His remarks followed Trump's warning about further U.S. action if a war-ending agreement is not reached.
  • Venezuela: Acting leader promises elections without a date. Acting President Delcy Rodríguez told the U.N. General Assembly Wednesday that Venezuela would hold elections during a transition to democracy but did not give a timetable. The absence of a date leaves the next step uncertain.
  • Pakistan and Afghanistan: Cross-border strikes intensify. Pakistan said Thursday that it struck 10 sites in Afghanistan that it said were used to store and launch drones. Afghanistan disputes Pakistan's broader allegations about militants sheltering across the border, leaving the risk of further clashes high.

Technology Industry News

  • Google releases Chrome 154 with 108 security fixes. The desktop browser update announced Tuesday includes fixes for several critical flaws and is rolling out to Windows, Mac and Linux users over the coming days and weeks.
  • Adobe patches critical business-software flaws. Adobe's September 22 update for Experience Manager Forms addresses vulnerabilities that could allow code execution, privilege escalation or security bypass. Adobe said it was not aware of exploitation in the wild.
  • Qualcomm unveils new flagship smartphone chips. The Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 and 8 Elite Gen 6, announced Tuesday, use a 2-nanometer process and are expected in upcoming premium Android phones. The launch sets the next hardware platform for multiple manufacturers.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • OpenAI releases GPT-6 Sol and Luna. OpenAI added the two models to its API on Tuesday, describing Sol as suited to complex coding and Luna as a lower-cost option for focused, high-volume tasks. The release expands the range of model costs and capabilities available to developers.
  • Anthropic introduces Claude Opus 5.5. The company said Tuesday that its new model performs at roughly the level of Claude Fable 5.1 on most work while costing 40% less to run than Opus 5. Those are company-reported comparisons.
  • Anthropic reports early AI-assisted biology finding. On Wednesday, its research team said Claude helped identify a previously uncharacterized enzyme system with DNA repeats resembling those seen in CRISPR systems. The system's function remains unknown, and the work is early research rather than a medical treatment.

Medicare & Medical News for Seniors

No verified changes to Medicare Parts A, B, or D were identified during the four-day reporting window.

  • Medicare bundled-care evaluation reports savings. A CMS evaluation released September 23 found that its Bundled Payments for Care Improvement Advanced model produced more than $800 million in net Medicare savings across eight model years. This is a health-system program result, not a new patient benefit; CMS said the model did not meet its criteria for expansion.
  • Alzheimer's research maps brain cells in new detail. On September 23, NIH described a large study mapping molecular activity across brain cell types in Alzheimer's and related disorders. The maps may guide future research, but they do not establish a new diagnostic test or treatment for patients.

This section is general news, not medical or insurance advice. Residents should confirm coverage with Medicare or their plan and discuss treatment decisions with a licensed clinician.
