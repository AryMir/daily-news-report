U.S. National News

  • Federal judge again pauses mail-ballot order. U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani blocked implementation of President Donald Trump's executive order limiting mail voting for two weeks, shortly before initial midterm ballots are due to be sent. The dispute is likely to return to higher courts, while the order's underlying legality remains unresolved.
  • Parties shift from primaries to the general-election campaign. With primaries ending, Democrats and Republicans are moving into a two-month push toward November, with candidates emphasizing affordability, health care, fuel prices and ideological differences in competitive districts.
  • Canada tariff move faces an untested legal path. The administration's new tariffs on Canada rely on Section 338, a provision that has not previously been used or tested in court. That creates legal uncertainty as the trade dispute with a major U.S. partner escalates.
  • Inflation and rate policy remain central economic questions. The latest personal-consumption-expenditures inflation reading remained elevated, while Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh said additional rate increases may be necessary. The combination keeps borrowing costs and household purchasing power in focus.
  • Iran war reaches six-month mark. The U.S.-Israel conflict with Iran has continued beyond early expectations, and the administration is placing greater emphasis on sanctions and pressure on countries that trade with Tehran. The prolonged conflict has raised concerns about costs and munitions availability.

International News

  • Russia resumes drone barrage around Kyiv, Ukraine. Russian attacks struck apartment buildings and warehouses in the Kyiv region for a second consecutive day. Ukrainian officials say repeated long air raids are disrupting civilian life and damaging infrastructure as the war continues.
  • Gaza strikes test a fragile ceasefire. Israeli strikes killed five people in Gaza and three in the West Bank on Friday, according to officials and hospitals. Israel said it was targeting militants, while the incidents added pressure to an already fragile ceasefire effort.
  • South Korea, Japan and the United States plan trilateral drill. Seoul said the three countries will conduct a military exercise next month in response to North Korean nuclear threats. North Korea criticized the planned drill and said it would respond to what it described as U.S. hostility.
  • Niger says it contained an attempted mutiny. Authorities said normal activity resumed in Niamey after young soldiers staged a challenge to the ruling junta. Reports said loyalist forces, with Russian support, repelled the effort, underscoring Niger's political and security instability.
  • Ebola outbreak expands in eastern Congo. Government figures showed the outbreak spread to two additional health zones, bringing 60 areas into the response. Authorities reported 5,794 confirmed cases and 2,786 deaths, while difficult security and displacement conditions continue to hamper containment.

Technology Industry News

  • Nvidia's outlook lifts chip makers. Nvidia projected revenue growth above analysts' expectations, helping ease investor concerns about data-center spending. Its shares rallied and several other semiconductor companies also advanced after the forecast.
  • Salesforce results revive software shares. Salesforce's results and outlook helped drive a sharp rebound in its stock, signaling stronger demand for business software after a period of pressure on the sector. The move also lifted broader software-industry sentiment.
  • CrowdStrike raises its outlook after revenue growth. The cybersecurity company reported fiscal second-quarter revenue growth of 26% from a year earlier, above estimates, and raised its annual net-new recurring-revenue outlook. Its results highlight continued spending on cloud and endpoint security.
  • Meta settles state claims over teen safety. Meta agreed to pay up to $18 billion over a decade and to limit teen use of Facebook and Instagram under a settlement with nearly all U.S. states. The agreement includes default usage limits and overnight restrictions unless parents consent.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • Judge overturns Pentagon designation of Anthropic. A federal judge ruled that the government's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk was unlawful and vacated directives against the company. The case concerns the government's ability to restrict use of commercial AI systems.
  • European AI Act moves into a key enforcement phase. A major enforcement deadline has arrived under the European Union's AI Act, pushing companies to put the bloc's requirements into operational practice. The rollout will test whether broad AI rules can be applied consistently across products and providers.
  • More than 100 companies call for AI cyber defenses. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft and other organizations signed an open letter urging coordinated preparation for AI-enabled cyberattacks. The group called for stronger defenses and cooperation across companies and governments.
