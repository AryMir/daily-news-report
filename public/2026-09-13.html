U.S. National News

Judge rejects planned 50% reduction in FEMA staffing. A federal judge ruled late Friday that the Department of Homeland Security's plan to cut the disaster-response agency's workforce in half was unlawful. The decision came in a broader lawsuit by federal labor groups challenging administration workforce reductions.

Georgia ballot-secrecy concern draws renewed scrutiny. Security researchers say Georgia's voting equipment can potentially allow a cast ballot to be matched to the voter, although the weakness cannot change votes or undermine vote totals. Election officials say mitigation steps are in place, while advocates argue that faster advances in artificial intelligence make a fuller fix more urgent before November.

Appeals court overturns order keeping Michigan coal plant open. A unanimous federal appeals panel ruled that the Energy Department exceeded its emergency authority when it required the 64-year-old J.H. Campbell plant to operate beyond its planned retirement. Financial filings indicate that continued operation has cost about $259 million, with the expense likely to be borne by Midwestern customers.

Record cyclospora outbreak declared over. Federal health officials said the largest recorded U.S. outbreak of the parasitic illness has ended and recalled iceberg lettuce linked to it is no longer on the market. Nearly 13,000 reported cases were associated with the outbreak, but investigators have not determined how the produce became contaminated.

Nation commemorates 25 years since the September 11 attacks. Memorials in New York, at the Pentagon, and in Pennsylvania honored nearly 3,000 people killed in 2001, along with survivors and first responders. The CIA also released dozens of declassified presidential intelligence briefs concerning Osama bin Laden and al-Qaida before and immediately after the attacks.

International News

Russian strikes kill eight people across Ukraine. Russian drone and missile attacks killed eight civilians and wounded dozens in several Ukrainian regions, including Zaporizhzhia, Kryvyi Rih, and Odesa. President Donald Trump separately urged Ukraine to halt attacks on Russian diesel infrastructure, while Moscow said it remained open to renewed autumn peace talks involving Russia, Ukraine, and the United States.

Sweden votes in closely contested parliamentary election. More than 8 million eligible voters are choosing a 349-seat parliament that will determine whether Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson's center-right government remains in power or the center-left returns. The Sweden Democrats could receive Cabinet posts for the first time if the right retains a majority.

Search expands after Indonesian ferry overturns. At least 129 people remained missing Sunday after the Virgo Transport 8 capsized in rough weather in the Java Sea. Authorities reported six deaths and at least 108 rescues from the ferry, which carried more than 240 people between East Java and South Kalimantan.

Congo Ebola outbreak spreads to a seventh province. Authorities confirmed the Bundibugyo strain in Sud-Ubangi, marking the outbreak's first spread into western Congo. Government figures reported 7,022 cases and 3,398 deaths as of Thursday, while insecurity, displacement, and health-worker strikes continue to hinder containment.

Saudi Arabia faces widening threats to oil routes. Houthi advances near the Bab al-Mandab Strait, drone attacks blamed on Iraqi militias, and continued disruption in the Strait of Hormuz are placing pressure on Saudi export routes. The overlapping threats have unsettled energy markets and narrowed the kingdom's options for moving oil safely.

Technology Industry News

ClickFix malware attacks spread across Windows and macOS. Attackers are compromising legitimate websites and displaying fake CAPTCHA instructions that persuade visitors to paste malicious commands into Windows or Mac terminals. Security researchers say the technique has become common because it is inexpensive, effective, and can evade familiar trust signals.

Android adds direct transfers between password managers. A new Google Play Services feature can move passwords and passkeys on-device between supported apps without creating an unencrypted CSV file. Google Password Manager, 1Password, Bitwarden, and Dashlane support the Credential Transfer API initially, with more providers expected later.

Leading chipmakers commit to ASML's next-generation lithography. Samsung and TSMC plan to adopt high-numerical-aperture extreme-ultraviolet equipment in coming years and have joined Intel in supporting larger photomasks. The change could raise the productivity of machines costing as much as $400 million by about 40%.

LinkedIn wins dismissal of browser-extension privacy suits. A federal judge dismissed two proposed class actions because the plaintiffs did not show that LinkedIn collected private information from extensions installed in their own browsers. The plaintiffs may amend their complaints, and one lawyer said other legal avenues remain under review.

Artificial Intelligence News

Anthropic chief calls for slower frontier-model development. CEO Dario Amodei said AI labs should give safety measures time to catch up with rapidly improving capabilities and allow stronger external scrutiny. Leaders at other major AI companies expressed support for parts of the proposal as concern grows over autonomous-agent behavior.

Senators debate a legal duty of care for AI developers. Bipartisan negotiators are considering legislation that would require developers of the most powerful models to mitigate known catastrophic risks. The proposal under discussion would also establish a court-involved process for the federal government to block release of a model judged unsafe.

Anthropic details state and criminal misuse of Claude. A new threat-intelligence report describes actors using AI for influence operations, cyber activity, surveillance, and attempts involving biological or conventional weapons. Anthropic said it banned identified accounts, improved detection systems, and coordinated with industry and government partners.

OpenAI backs mandatory national safety rules. The company called for capability-based federal regulation and announced support for four California bills covering independent safety assessments, AI-auditor standards, protections for young people, and safeguards against AI-enabled biological threats. The position reflects growing industry acceptance of formal oversight for advanced systems.
