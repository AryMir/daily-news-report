2026-07-14

Executive Summary

  • The U.S. launched fresh strikes on Iran and Tehran retaliated across the Middle East, putting the Strait of Hormuz back at the center of global security and energy worries.
  • In U.S. news, investigations continued after a fatal ICE shooting in Maine, press-freedom groups denounced new subpoenas to New York Times reporters, and two Supreme Court justices made a rare appearance before Congress over security funding.
  • Internationally, the death toll from a Bangkok music bar fire rose to 30, while China reported a 27% jump in June exports helped by strong AI-related demand.
  • Markets were mixed but generally steadier after a softer U.S. inflation report, while crude oil stayed elevated on Middle East supply concerns.
  • Henderson is starting another very hot day, with current conditions near 92°F, a forecast high around 105°F, and a slight chance of late-day thunderstorms.

U.S. National News

  • South Carolina Gov. Henry McMaster chose Darline Graham Nordone, Sen. Lindsey Graham's sister, to fill the remainder of Graham's Senate term. The appointment gives Republicans short-term continuity after Graham's sudden death over the weekend.
  • Investigations continued after an ICE officer fatally shot a Colombian motorist in Biddeford, Maine. AP reported the man was not the target of the warrant officers were serving, and protests have grown over the use of force and lack of body-camera footage.
  • Free-press groups condemned Justice Department subpoenas issued to five New York Times reporters over stories about security concerns involving the Qatari-gifted Air Force One. The dispute is becoming a broader test of press protections in Trump's second term.
  • Supreme Court Justices Elena Kagan and Amy Coney Barrett appeared before Congress to seek additional security funding as threats against judges keep rising. The court is asking for a larger budget to bolster personal protection and emergency response.
  • A dangerous heat wave is still gripping much of the country, with AP reporting that many cities could set record overnight temperatures. Forecasters warn that the lack of nighttime cooling sharply increases health risks, especially for older adults and people without air conditioning.

International News

  • The U.S. launched new strikes on Iran early Tuesday, and Tehran responded with attacks on U.S.-aligned countries and ships near the Strait of Hormuz. The renewed fighting threatens to unravel the interim truce and raises the risk of a broader regional war.
  • The death toll from the Bangkok music bar fire rose to 30, with dozens of people still hospitalized. Thai officials ordered broader safety inspections as investigators looked at possible problems with exits, wiring and flammable interior materials.
  • China's customs agency said June exports jumped 27% from a year earlier, far above expectations. Officials said strong demand for AI-related electronic components and computing hardware was a major driver.
  • Fourteen countries and the European Union reaffirmed the 2016 ruling that found no legal basis for China's sweeping South China Sea claims. Their statements also criticized coercive actions in the disputed waters and renewed pressure on Beijing to comply with international law.
  • The U.N. refugee agency is investigating reports that two boats carrying Rohingya refugees capsized in the Bay of Bengal after leaving Myanmar. The case underscores worsening conditions for Rohingya families, many of whom are fleeing conflict and aid cuts in search of safety.

Technology Industry News

  • IBM shares tumbled more than 23% in premarket trading after the company said preliminary second-quarter revenue and adjusted profit would miss Wall Street expectations. CEO Arvind Krishna said some large deals slipped and clients shifted spending toward servers, storage and memory.
  • Japan's space agency, JAXA, completed the first test flight of its experimental reusable RV-X rocket. The short hover-and-land mission is an early but important step in Japan's effort to cut launch costs and compete more directly in the global commercial space market.
  • SK Hynix's recent Wall Street debut continues to stand out as one of the clearest signs of how strongly chip demand is being tied to AI infrastructure. The South Korean memory maker raised $26.5 billion and surged nearly 13% in its first U.S. session.
  • China's latest trade report showed exports of electronic components and computing hardware up nearly 57% in the first half of the year. That points to continued heavy global demand for servers, semiconductors and other technology gear.

Artificial Intelligence News

  • More than 200 economists, AI researchers and technology executives signed an open letter urging governments and institutions to act now on AI's economic impact. The group warned that job displacement could come quickly and that policy frameworks should not lag the technology.
  • SoftBank CEO Masayoshi Son said the world may need nearly $5 trillion a year of investment to keep up with AI demand. He rejected the idea that current AI spending amounts to a speculative bubble.
  • AP reported that the AI infrastructure boom is now contributing to inflation by pushing up the cost of semiconductors, memory, electricity and consumer electronics. That is becoming part of the Federal Reserve's policy debate.
  • New York is preparing the country's first statewide moratorium on new large data centers for up to a year while it develops rules on energy use, water consumption and environmental effects. The move shows how quickly AI buildouts are colliding with state utility and land-use policy.
  • Utilities, regulators and clean-energy advocates are also fighting over how to power AI data centers. Several states are weighing whether new AI electricity demand should be matched more directly with renewable energy or other dedicated supply.
